## Provisional conclusion

Constellation Software has a durable conventional franchise based on embedded niche workflows, customer relationships and decentralized domain expertise. The public evidence supports specific AI capabilities and, in some cases, management-reported operational use. It does not provide a controlled, quality-adjusted company result tied to retained incremental cash. The central underwriting issue is capture: operational improvement may pass to customers, be absorbed by implementation, already sit in the reported baseline, or be dominated by cycle and mix. The dossier therefore keeps G2 provisional and treats E3/E4 as unestablished.

## Conventional business and financial anchor

Constellation Software reports hundreds of vertical-market-software businesses grouped under operating groups including Vela and Volaris. Customer value arises from dependable execution, integration and domain expertise, not data volume alone. Competitive advantage must survive Roper Technologies and generic model vendors and new vertical SaaS products. Switching costs and distribution help, but contracts, interoperability and procurement can reallocate gains.

For FY2025, the opened [issuer results](https://www.csisoftware.com/constellation-software-inc-announces-results-for-the-fourth-quarter-and-year-ended-december-31-2025-and-declares-quarterly-dividend/) identify **$11.623bn revenue**, **$512m net income attributable to common shareholders**, **$2.732bn cash flow from operations**, and **$1.683bn FCFA2S**, a non-IFRS measure. The after-tax attributable-income line governs the hurdle; FCFA2S is context only. Acquisitions drive much of the portfolio’s growth and confound simple organic comparisons.

The [Q2 2026 issuer announcement](https://www.csisoftware.com/) reports quarterly revenue of $3.335bn, up 17%, with net income attributable to common shareholders of $274m; organic growth was only 3%, so acquisitions drove most growth. Constellation discloses a technology-resilience posture but no group-wide named AI production deployment or measured outcome. The scoped AI mechanisms therefore remain E0/E1 hypotheses, not conditional E2.

The ordinary baseline already contains deployed analytics, automation and machine learning. Capital allocation must fund maintenance, growth investment, obligations and shareholder distributions before any incremental AI benefit. Financial scale permits experimentation, but it does not demonstrate quality, adoption or capture.

## Measurement contract and retained economics

Pre-register **verified tasks per user/support hour, gross retention, net revenue per customer and contribution after compute and seat cannibalization**. Compare randomized or carefully matched sites, clients, products or cohorts against the current production system, including existing ML. Define the eligible workload before rollout; hold service, safety, accuracy, risk and customer-mix quality constant. Report sample size, duration, confidence interval, adoption, exception/rework rate and all human/model/implementation cost. The known public result is insufficient for E3. A future annual report, product cohort study or customer renewal disclosure linking the KPI to contribution could close the gap.

The framework's annual research convention is **$25.6m**: 5% × FY2025 attributable net income of $512m, after tax. The illustrative pre-tax bridge is $1.0bn eligible labor ×15%×50%×50%−$20m = $17.5m; applying an explicit 25% research tax assumption gives **$13.125m after tax**, below the hurdle. Revenue effects must subtract seat and service cannibalization.

## Competing hypotheses, falsifiers and gates

**Strongest positive hypothesis:** proprietary workflow context and distribution let Constellation Software deploy validated AI faster than standalone vendors, with better feedback and a retained service, pricing, throughput or capital benefit. **Strongest negative hypothesis:** model capability commoditizes, customer rights prevent pooling, implementation and verification absorb gains, and Roper Technologies or generic model vendors and new vertical SaaS products forces pass-through.

Three falsifiers are: (1) matched deployments fail to improve verified tasks per user/support hour, gross retention, net revenue per customer and contribution after compute and seat cannibalization at equal quality; (2) five-year retained benefit after recurring AI and implementation cost remains below the stated 5% hurdle; (3) contracts, portability or competitor performance show that the alleged data/workflow advantage is readily replicated. Unresolved questions are eligible-workload share, exact production penetration, customer-level retention/pricing, recurring model cost, contract training rights and the latest post-FY2025 adverse development.

G0 passes because asset, mechanism, capture route and falsifiers are explicit. G1 is provisionally supported by the conventional franchise and financial capacity, subject to latest-period refresh and sector risks. G2 remains **open**: scoped E1/E2 can support further testing, but E3/E4 are absent. G3 and G4 are **deferred by user**. Confidence is **medium** for mechanism, **low-to-medium** for deployment breadth and **low** for capture.
