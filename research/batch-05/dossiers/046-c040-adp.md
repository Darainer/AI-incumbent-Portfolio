## Provisional conclusion

ADP has a durable conventional franchise based on payroll system of record, employer distribution, tax/regulatory content and service history. The public evidence supports specific AI capabilities and, in some cases, management-reported operational use. It does not provide a controlled, quality-adjusted company result tied to retained incremental cash. The central underwriting issue is capture: operational improvement may pass to customers, be absorbed by implementation, already sit in the reported baseline, or be dominated by cycle and mix. The dossier therefore keeps G2 provisional and treats E3/E4 as unestablished.

## Conventional business and financial anchor

ADP reports Employer Services and PEO Services, supported by payroll, tax, benefits and compliance workflows. Customer value arises from dependable execution, integration and domain expertise, not data volume alone. Competitive advantage must survive Paychex and Rippling and other unified HR/payroll SaaS platforms. Switching costs and distribution help, but contracts, interoperability and procurement can reallocate gains.

For FY2025 ended 30 June 2025, the opened company reporting identifies **about $20.6bn revenue**, **about $5.4bn adjusted EBIT; reported operating profit is lower and should be used for statutory reconciliation**, and **approximately $5bn operating cash flow, with low physical capex relative to payroll-float and software economics**. The capital context is that client funds obligations and associated assets must be separated from corporate debt. Reported and adjusted measures are not interchangeable: the materiality bridge below uses the explicitly labelled profit measure or proxy and does not present it as normalized earnings. Acquisitions, currency, product/geographic mix, restructuring, commodity or equipment cycles, and accounting changes can confound comparisons. The latest annual-report/results hub should be reopened for exact statement lines during lead review.

The [FY2026 investor release hub](https://investors.adp.com/overview/default.aspx) identifies Q4/FY2026 as the latest completed period. FY2026 revenue was $21.95bn, up 7%. ADP states more than 1.1m clients in 140+ countries and describes AI-driven insights; its public product claims support availability, but no matched payroll-accuracy, service-capacity or cash-capture result was found.

The ordinary baseline already contains deployed analytics, automation and machine learning. Capital allocation must fund maintenance, growth investment, obligations and shareholder distributions before any incremental AI benefit. Financial scale permits experimentation, but it does not demonstrate quality, adoption or capture.

## Measurement contract and retained economics

Pre-register **accurate payrolls per service hour, first-contact resolution, retention, net revenue per client and AI contribution after model cost**. Compare randomized or carefully matched sites, clients, products or cohorts against the current production system, including existing ML. Define the eligible workload before rollout; hold service, safety, accuracy, risk and customer-mix quality constant. Report sample size, duration, confidence interval, adoption, exception/rework rate and all human/model/implementation cost. The known public result is insufficient for E3. A future annual report, product cohort study or customer renewal disclosure linking the KPI to contribution could close the gap.

The framework's five-year research convention is **$221m** for this packet. 5%×$4.41bn FY2026 net earnings=$220.5m. A $4bn eligible service/development pool×12%×60%×60%−$50m=$123m; pricing, PEO pass-through and released capacity must be reconciled. Assumption inputs are illustrative and reproducible. They are neither guidance nor valuation. Released time counts only if removed or redeployed to valued output; the same capacity cannot be counted as both cost saving and revenue. Capital, working capital and tax would need separate treatment.

## Competing hypotheses, falsifiers and gates

**Strongest positive hypothesis:** proprietary workflow context and distribution let ADP deploy validated AI faster than standalone vendors, with better feedback and a retained service, pricing, throughput or capital benefit. **Strongest negative hypothesis:** model capability commoditizes, customer rights prevent pooling, implementation and verification absorb gains, and Paychex or Rippling and other unified HR/payroll SaaS platforms forces pass-through.

Three falsifiers are: (1) matched deployments fail to improve accurate payrolls per service hour, first-contact resolution, retention, net revenue per client and AI contribution after model cost at equal quality; (2) five-year retained benefit after recurring AI and implementation cost remains below the stated 5% hurdle; (3) contracts, portability or competitor performance show that the alleged data/workflow advantage is readily replicated. Unresolved questions are eligible-workload share, exact production penetration, customer-level retention/pricing, recurring model cost, contract training rights and the latest post-FY2026 adverse development.

G0 passes because asset, mechanism, capture route and falsifiers are explicit. G1 is provisionally supported by the conventional franchise and financial capacity, subject to latest-period refresh and sector risks. G2 remains **open**: scoped E1/E2 can support further testing, but E3/E4 are absent. G3 and G4 are **deferred by user**. Confidence is **medium** for mechanism, **low-to-medium** for deployment breadth and **low** for capture.
