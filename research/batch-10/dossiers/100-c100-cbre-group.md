# C100 — CBRE Group evidence dossier

**Unreviewed agent work.** CBRE Group; CBRE (NYSE, USD quote/reporting). Cutoff/retrieval 12 September 2026. property-services firm. Latest period: Q2 2026 ended 30 June, released 29 July 2026.

## Business and financial anchor

CBRE Group owns or operates commercial-property workflows. Its scarce asset is lease/sale/valuation comparables, building-operations telemetry, client relationships, 155bn AUM and execution rights across facilities/projects. AI could automate valuation/research and facilities/project workflows, improving accepted output and technician utilization while managing billable-hour/fee cannibalization. Existing statistical valuation, routing, building controls and portfolio analytics remain in the reported baseline.

Latest results: **$11.226bn revenue including $4.622bn pass-through costs**, **$836m core EBITDA; GAAP net income attributable $204m**, **TTM operating cash flow nearly $1.4bn and FCF nearly $1.7bn; quarterly FCF $29m**. Capital context: **$7.382bn debt, $1.489bn cash, $5.893bn net debt and 1.60x net leverage**. Sources: [latest results](https://www.cbre.com/press-releases/cbre-q2-2026-earnings) and [2025 annual report/10-K](https://ir.cbre.com/financial-reports/sec-filings/content/0001138118-26-000005/cbre-20251231.htm). Core FFO/core EBITDA/FCF/net debt are non-GAAP and require reconciliation. Development/disposition gains, incentive fees, acquisitions, occupancy, rent spreads, FX, pass-through costs and interest rates confound comparisons. 2025 transaction recovery, acquisitions, pass-through facilities costs, incentive compensation and real-estate cycle explain much growth; clients own building data and can demand savings.

The conventional business survives without incremental AI because location, buildings, customer access, regulatory licenses, capital and service execution are scarce. For a REIT, AFFO/maintenance capex, debt refinancing and tenant pass-through matter; for services, pass-through revenue, people costs and transactional cyclicality matter. Data-center leasing or project demand caused by AI is ordinary demand exposure, not proof of internal productivity.

## Rights audit

| Input | Owner/exclusivity | Permitted inference/training | Labels/quality | Replication threat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Leases, sales and valuations | landlords, tenants, public records, brokers | transaction/service use; confidentiality and database licenses limit pooling | closed prices/rents useful but sparse, selected and lagged | MSCI/CoStar, rival brokers and public records |
| Building sensors/work orders | building owner/occupier/vendor | facilities purpose under contract; cross-building training not presumed | frequent failures/energy labels, equipment heterogeneity | controls vendors and clients own source systems |
| Tenant/customer operations | tenant/customer | customer-specific service only unless expressly licensed | high context, commercially sensitive | clients can switch/multi-source providers |
| Geospatial/climate/energy data | public and licensed vendors | license field-of-use and redistribution constraints | broad coverage, model uncertainty | many vendors and open sources |
| Employee/technician activity | company/worker subject to law | staffing/safety use; monitoring and automated employment rules apply | completion/SLA labels, gaming and complexity bias | competitors have comparable records |

Possession through management or brokerage does not establish ownership or cross-client training permission. Lease confidentiality, privacy, building cybersecurity and upstream vendor licenses constrain use. Customer-specific inference can improve service without creating exclusive pooled learning.

## Mechanisms and challengers

1. **Leasing/valuation intelligence (classical ML/generative assistance).** Comparables and property context → rent/value/prospect recommendation → accepted transaction at maintained accuracy → fee, occupancy or rent economics. CBRE names Ellis AI as a self-service multi-model GenAI platform and describes use by teams for digital assistants, supporting scoped company-reported E2 deployment; no controlled incremental cash cohort was found.
2. **Facilities/predictive maintenance (classical ML/agentic execution).** Sensor/work-order history → predict, dispatch and close → lower downtime/energy/cost at equal safety → service margin or tenant retention. Much gain may flow to the occupier under contract; hardware and technician capacity constrain realization.
3. **Research/document automation (generative assistance).** Leases, market reports and project files → extract/draft → professional-approved output → lower labor or more mandates. Human liability/review remains. For service firms, fewer billable hours and client fee resets can offset productivity.

Direct challenger: **JLL and Cushman & Wakefield**. CoStar/MSCI, building-control vendors and client in-house systems are substitutes. Hyperscalers/model vendors can capture compute/interface value. Open/public records and data portability reduce exclusivity. Customers may procure outcomes and demand pass-through.

## KPI and materiality

| KPI | Comparator | Eligible/quality | Known result | Closing evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Occupancy/rent spread or mandate conversion | matched properties/markets before model | model-used opportunities; concessions, risk and cycle controlled | unknown | versioned property cohort |
| Downtime/energy/work-order cost per sqft | similar buildings/current rules | connected buildings; comfort, safety and capex controlled | unknown | matched rollout and contract cash |
| Accepted reports/valuations per professional hour | prior workflow | AI users; error, audit and liability non-inferior | unknown | randomized team study |

The materiality convention is **$184m annual pre-tax using 5% of $3.680bn TTM core EBITDA only as a reported-profit scale proxy**. Illustration: **assumed $4bn eligible labor cost × 15% × 60% × 40% − $120m = $24m**. Inputs are assumptions. Tenant energy savings are not landlord/service-provider cost unless contractually retained. Released professional capacity cannot also be counted as labor removal and revenue. Property capex, maintenance, tax and working capital must be separate.

Positive: embedded property/customer access and physical execution let analytics change real outcomes. Negative: rights are shared, cycles dominate, clients/vendors capture savings and physical work limits scaling.

Falsifiers: matched properties show no risk-adjusted rent/occupancy or service-cost improvement; predictive maintenance raises capex/false alarms without downtime gain; client renewals pass through savings or generative tools reduce fees/hours faster than costs. Confidence mechanism **medium**, deployment **low-medium**, capture **low**. G0/G1 provisional; G2 incomplete without controlled quality and retained cash. G3/G4 deferred.
