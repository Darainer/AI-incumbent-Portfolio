## Provisional conclusion and conventional business

SLB has a credible incumbent-AI mechanism because it controls subsurface data, physics models, field equipment, domain expertise and execution inside customer workflows. The scoped evidence assessment is **E1 digital/AI capability; no opened retained-economics cohort**. It does **not** establish controlled quality-adjusted improvement across a representative workload or incremental cash retained by shareholders. The investment question therefore remains operational: can management disclose a stable baseline, eligible workload, quality guardrail, realized economics and recurring technology cost?

The latest conventional anchor is [Q2 2026 (ended 30 June 2026)](https://investorcenter.slb.com/news-releases/news-release-details/2026/slb-announces-second-quarter-2026-results/): **$8.972bn revenue, up about 5%** and **$786m net income; roughly 15.6% operating margin reported by market summaries**. These figures are reported unless explicitly labelled adjusted. $1.4bn operating cash flow and $716m free cash flow. $5bn committed credit facilities were reportedly undrawn; debt and acquisition integration remain claims on cash. The principal confounders are cycle, mix, pricing, acquisitions/restructuring, working capital and capital intensity. Existing prediction, optimization and automation are already in these reported results; only incremental improvement from here belongs in an AI bridge.

## AI mechanisms and competitive challenge

**Mechanism 1 — scoped workflow improvement.** Lumi/data platforms and digital drilling can optimize well planning and operations; production evidence exists, but customer economics and attribution to AI versus automation are thin.

**Mechanism 2 — execution or adjacent demand.** data-center solutions are external infrastructure demand and should not be counted as SLB internal productivity; they add revenue with distinct capital/margin economics.

**Challenger and substitute.** Halliburton and Baker Hughes hold competing workflows/equipment; cloud vendors and operators can internalize digital layers.

**Adverse evidence.** oil cycles, offshore mix and ChampionX integration dominate results; customers own much field data and can negotiate savings away.

## Measurement contract and materiality arithmetic

The framework’s research convention is **$250m, roughly 5% of a normalized $5bn annual operating-income scale proxy**. This is a scale test, not a forecast or valuation.

| KPI / unit | Baseline and comparator | Eligible workload | Quality condition | Known result / next proof event |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Drilling time/cost per quality foot | matched basin/well design | digital-enabled wells | same safety and reservoir result | operator-verified cohort |
| Digital ARR/contribution/customer | pre-Lumi contract | production digital accounts | after cloud, integration and support | renewal cohort disclosure |

Illustrative cost bridge: `$6bn eligible engineering, field-service and digital operations cost/revenue pool × 12% task efficiency × 40% realization × 50% retained share − $100m recurring AI expense = $44m annual operating benefit`. All bridge inputs are assumptions. The result is below the hurdle. It excludes customer savings; any revenue case must use incremental company contribution after cannibalization, servicing and model cost. Capex and working capital remain separate.

## Hypotheses, falsifiers and evidence gates

**Strongest positive hypothesis:** the installed asset and distribution let SLB embed models in a difficult workflow, observe outcomes and retain part of better throughput, decisions or service. **Strongest negative hypothesis:** generic models and rivals reach the same customer context, contractual rights block pooled learning, and customers, workers or suppliers capture the gain while implementation/capital cost remains with the company.

Three falsifiers: (1) by the next two annual reporting cycles, management still cannot disclose active production penetration and a quality-adjusted KPI for a material workload; (2) renewals, usage or contribution margin deteriorate as AI pricing, cannibalization and supplier costs rise; (3) matched rollouts fail to beat the existing production system after errors, rework, safety/risk and implementation cost. Core-business deterioration must be diagnosed separately from AI failure.

Unresolved questions are the precise customer-data licenses; whether feedback may improve a pooled model; eligible workload; representative baseline; error and escalation rate; incremental price/volume; labor realization; recurring inference/support cost; required capex; and the share of savings retained after competition or regulation.

**G0:** provisionally satisfied: asset, mechanisms, capture hypothesis and falsifiers are explicit. **G1:** provisionally satisfied only subject to the stated balance-sheet, governance and rights gaps. **G2:** provisional **watch / incomplete**: E1 digital/AI capability; no opened retained-economics cohort; E3 quality-adjusted evidence and E4 retained cash are absent. **G3/G4:** deferred by user. Confidence is **moderate** in mechanism, **low-to-moderate** in broad deployment and **low** in capture.
