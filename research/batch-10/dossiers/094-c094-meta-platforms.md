# Meta Platforms — evidence packet

**Identity.** Meta Platforms, Inc., Nasdaq:META, USD. Cutoff 12 September 2026; latest period Q2 2026 ended 30 June. AI is scoped to advertising ranking/creation, feed/recommendation, business messaging and model/platform products. Reality Labs is treated separately. G3/G4 are deferred.

## Business and financial baseline

Meta's Family of Apps includes Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, WhatsApp and Threads. The business monetizes attention primarily through advertising. Its assets are billions of daily users, advertiser demand, interaction and conversion signals, global delivery infrastructure and integrated auction/ranking systems. Google, TikTok, Amazon and other platforms compete for advertiser budgets and user time; Apple and mobile platforms control important signals and distribution. Open model availability and rising compute requirements can weaken capture.

Q2 2026 revenue was **$60.801bn**, advertising revenue **$59.363bn**, operating income **$18.775bn**, net income **$15.848bn** and operating margin 31%. Average Family daily active people were 3.60bn. Ad impressions rose 14% and average price per ad rose 12% year over year. Operating cash flow was **$31.862bn**, while capital expenditures including finance-lease principal were **$31.078bn**, leaving only **$784m free cash flow**. Cash and marketable securities were $90.26bn and long-term debt $83.66bn. [Meta Q2 results](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx), 29 July 2026.

Costs grew 55%, including $2.4bn of legal charges and $1.18bn of severance. Meta forecast 2026 capex of $130–145bn. These facts make attribution difficult: revenue growth can reflect demand, pricing, impressions, FX and product mix, while operating-income comparison includes legal and restructuring items. The pre-tax operating-income anchor and a pre-tax scenario are kept dimensionally aligned; the free-cash-flow collapse is a separate capital-intensity constraint.

## Mechanisms and evidence

**Ad selection and auction ranking.** User/context signals, advertiser creative and conversion feedback enter prediction and ranking models; the system selects an ad and price; the economic outputs are advertiser return, auction demand, impressions and price. Meta reported 14% more impressions and 12% higher price per ad, but did not isolate AI's counterfactual share. These are business outcomes with multiple causes, not controlled AI evidence.

**Generative ad creation and targeting.** Models create or vary images/text and automate audience/campaign choices. Meta published a company study saying new AI-driven tools improved advertiser return on ad spend by 22%, comparing $4.52 revenue per dollar for users of new tools with $3.71 for other advertising, and says the work included a large randomized controlled trial using established academic methods. [Meta ads study](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/05/over-half-trillion-dollars-3-million-us-jobs-linked-metas-ai-driven-ads-technologies/), 12 May 2025. This is an E2 company-reported advertiser outcome. The press page says the broader figures are grounded in a randomized trial, but its linked study redirected to Facebook login and the SSRN paper returned 403; the accessible material does not verify that the 22% AI-tool uplift itself was randomized against the relevant baseline. E3 therefore remains unverified. The claim combines tools/methods and does not expose leakage, incrementality or Meta-margin effects. Advertiser revenue is not Meta revenue.

**Recommendation and engagement.** Ranking models select feed, Reels and suggested content; better relevance may increase time, ad inventory and retention. More engagement can also increase integrity, safety and regulatory costs. A causal test must report holdout engagement plus well-being, policy violations, user retention and incremental ad contribution. Management statements that AI improves recommendations do not alone establish retained cash.

**Business messaging and assistants.** Models can automate customer support or commerce in WhatsApp/Messenger and enable new paid tools. Public disclosure does not isolate paying AI-agent customers, renewal, inference cost or cannibalization. Open-source model releases may support ecosystem adoption but can diffuse technical advantage and incur cost without direct revenue.

Meta controls first-party interactions on its services subject to user terms, privacy law, purpose limitation and deletion/consent obligations. Advertiser conversion data can depend on customer systems, pixels, mobile identifiers and clean-room contracts. Apple and browser restrictions reduce observability. Licensed content and public-web training face rights and litigation questions. Creators and users supply scarce content and can capture value through revenue sharing or leave if ranking quality deteriorates. NVIDIA and infrastructure suppliers capture spending; regulators can constrain personalization.

## KPI contract and materiality

Primary contract: **incremental advertising contribution profit per exposed impression** in persistent randomized holdouts of AI ranking/creative tools. Report advertiser conversion value, Meta revenue, impressions, price, user retention, policy violations, inference expense, traffic-acquisition/revenue sharing and confidence intervals. Separate ranking from creative generation and new inventory. Secondary KPIs are paying business-agent accounts, net retention and contribution margin; recommendation minutes must carry safety and satisfaction guardrails.

Five percent of annualized Q2 operating income is **$3.755bn/year** (`$18.775bn×4×5%`). Illustration: annualized Q2 advertising revenue $237.452bn × 2% incremental AI-attributable revenue ×45% contribution margin ×80% retention after ecosystem/measurement leakage = **$1.710bn**, less $1.0bn incremental recurring model/inference/safety expense, net **$0.710bn/year**. Required incremental ad-revenue uplift holding other assumptions is `(3.755+1.0)/(237.452×.45×.80)=5.56%`. Inputs are scenarios, not guidance. Both sides are pre-tax operating amounts. Capex is not deducted again in this EBIT bridge; its effect is separately visible in FCF and later depreciation, avoiding double counting.

Positive: first-party feedback, auction scale and direct experimentation allow rapid improvement and monetization. Negative: mobile/privacy constraints, rising compute, safety/regulatory costs, open-model diffusion and customer/creator bargaining can erode retention. Falsifiers: holdouts show no incremental contribution, ROAS gains do not increase spend, user/safety guardrails worsen, agent products lack paying retention, or normalized FCF remains compressed despite revenue growth. Questions: persistent holdout uplift, inference cost per ad/recommendation, incremental capex utilization, advertiser cohorts, rights and safety cost.

Confidence mechanism high; deployment high; capture medium. G0 pass; G1 pass/provisional because strong OCF and liquidity coexist with extreme capex and rising debt; G2 provisional on E2 company-reported advertiser outcome and clear production scale; E3 and group retained-cash increment remain unproven. G3/G4 deferred.
