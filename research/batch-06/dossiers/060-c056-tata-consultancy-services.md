# C056 — Tata Consultancy Services evidence dossier

**Unreviewed agent work.** Tata Consultancy Services; ordinary equity TCS (NSE/BSE India, INR statutory reporting; ADR absent). Cutoff/retrieval 12 September 2026. Latest period: Q1 FY2027 ended 30 June 2026, published 9 July 2026.

## Business and financial anchor

Tata Consultancy Services sells consulting, systems integration, application development, managed services and business-process operations. Revenue largely reflects people and subcontractor time under time-and-materials, fixed-price or managed-service contracts. Client relationships, domain process knowledge, delivery centers and partner certifications are the conventional assets. AI can raise delivery output, but the same gain can reduce billable hours, shorten projects or invite client price resets. Therefore bookings and “AI revenue” are not retained shareholder economics.

Latest Q1 FY2027 revenue was **$7.624bn**, adjusted operating income **$1.826bn (24.0% margin)**, adjusted net income **$1.460bn**, and net cash from operations **$1.310bn**. The asterisked profit measures exclude an exceptional item. Annualized AI revenue was $2.6bn, a company-defined run rate rather than recognized annual revenue. Primary sources: [latest results](https://www.tcs.com/who-we-are/newsroom/press-release/tcs-financial-results-q1-fy-2027) and [Q1 fact sheet](https://www.tcs.com/content/dam/tcs/investor-relations/financial-statements/2026-27/q1/Presentations/Q1%202026-27%20Fact%20Sheet.pdf) and [annual filings/results](https://www.tcs.com/investor-relations/financial-statements). Reported versus adjusted margins, currency, acquisitions, severance and utilization must be separated. Management explicitly accepts that AI may cannibalize revenue; traditional effort-based work and client spending remain under pressure.

The business remains viable without incremental AI because large enterprises need accountable integration, security, change management and legacy modernization. Yet hyperscalers, software vendors, offshore rivals and internal client teams compete. Wage inflation, attrition, utilization, subcontractors, visa rules and discretionary spending drive ordinary margin. Cash is generally strong, but acquisitions and buybacks/dividends require the exact statutory cash/debt schedule; unavailable items are not inferred.

Possession during an engagement is not a lawful pooled-training right. Contract allocation of generated code, indemnity, open-source provenance, data residency and prompt retention determines use. Customer-specific retrieval can still be valuable without cross-client learning, but it is less exclusive.

## Mechanisms, challengers and evidence

1. **Software delivery copilot/agents (generative assistance/agentic execution).** Client code and requirements → draft code/tests/migration actions → engineer-approved deployable change → more output per team. Production use and training programs are E2 when company/customer reported. Lines of code or prompt counts are E1 activity; accepted cycle time and defect-controlled throughput are required for E3.
2. **Managed operations (agentic execution).** Tickets/process records → classify, resolve or orchestrate workflows → closed cases at maintained service levels → lower delivery cost or outcome fee. Human exceptions and model costs matter. Savings can be contractually passed through at renewal.
3. **AI transformation sales.** Distribution and partner expertise → advisory/integration projects → incremental contribution after hiring, subcontractors and model fees. annualized AI revenue of $2.6bn at Q1 FY2027 is reported; annualized run-rate is not audited annual revenue and scope taxonomy may change. It can coexist with erosion of legacy revenue.

The direct incumbent challenger is Accenture, with comparable global delivery and hyperscaler alliances. Substitutes are client internal teams, software vendors and AI coding/agent platforms selling directly. Clients own source systems and can insist on productivity pricing. Hyperscalers may capture model/compute margin. Smaller AI-native integrators can undercut day rates.

## KPI and materiality

| KPI | Baseline/comparator | Eligible/quality | Known result | Closing evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accepted story points/releases per team-month | matched pre-AI projects/similar stack | adopted teams; defects, security and rework non-inferior | company-wide unknown | randomized portfolio study |
| Cost per SLA-compliant closed ticket | existing automation/manual | eligible tickets; reopen, escalation and satisfaction controlled | unknown | contract cohort and margin bridge |
| AI contract gross margin net legacy erosion | same client/service baseline | AI-tagged contracts; lost hours, discounts, compute and reskilling included | revenue/bookings only | audited cohort and renewal economics |

The Q1 fact sheet reports $1.826bn adjusted operating income, excluding an exceptional item, so the same-quarter 5% hurdle is **$91.3m pre-tax**; it is not annualized. Break-even is `AI contract contribution + delivery savings retained − displaced legacy contribution − model/reskilling cost ≥ 5% of normalized annual operating profit`. Illustration on 100 profit units: $10bn eligible labor × 15% efficiency × 50% realization × 30% retention − $300m AI cost = **−$75m** (`10,000×.15×.50×.30−300`); the example is annual while the hurdle is quarterly, so it is **NOT_COMPARABLE** without time alignment; assumptions expose why billable-hour cannibalization and client sharing are decisive. A separate illustrative $2bn AI revenue at 25% contribution less $300m displaced legacy contribution produces $200m, but the reported annualized run-rate is not audited annual revenue and its scope taxonomy may change, so this is not treated as fact.

Positive: trusted integration and change-management access lets the firm deploy AI across complex estates and shift to outcomes. Negative: generic tools let clients internalize work, hours fall faster than outcome pricing grows, and vendors/clients capture savings.

Falsifiers: accepted-output productivity fails after defect controls; AI-client cohorts show lower total contribution after legacy erosion; renewal pricing passes most benefit to clients while model cost rises. Confidence mechanism **high**, deployment **medium**, capture **low**. G0/G1 provisional; G2 incomplete because commercial/deployment E2 lacks controlled retained economics. G3/G4 deferred.
