# TSMC — evidence packet

**Identity.** Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company Ltd., TWSE:2330 ordinary shares (TWD) and NYSE:TSM ADR (five ordinary shares per ADR), reporting TWD. Cutoff/retrieval 12 September 2026; latest Q2 2026 released 16 July, with August monthly revenue released 10 September. Unreviewed; G3/G4 deferred. AI is currently a powerful demand driver for TSMC capacity, which is distinct from AI improving TSMC’s own fabrication productivity. Public evidence for internal AI is qualitative and does not isolate yield/cash gains.

## Business/financial resilience

TSMC is the leading pure-play foundry, manufacturing customer designs across advanced and specialty nodes and advanced packaging. Process know-how, enormous capex, customer trust, yield learning and design ecosystem are scarce. Customer concentration, semiconductor cycles, Taiwan geopolitics, export controls, electricity/water and costly overseas fabs are risks. Samsung Foundry and Intel Foundry are challengers; customers may dual-source where feasible.

Q2 2026 revenue was about **NT$1.27tn**, gross margin **67.7%**, operating margin **60.3%**, and net income **NT$706.6bn**; free cash flow was **NT$287.36bn** as operating cash less capex. [Q2 results](https://investor.tsmc.com/english/quarterly-results/2026/q2), 16 July 2026, and [management report](https://investor.tsmc.com/chinese/encrypt/files/encrypt_file/reports/2026-07/6f49632674bd2d0fd48cb65aaf89ec6ab510b559/2Q26%20ManagementReport.pdf). 2026 capex guidance rose to US$60–64bn, and overseas expansion pressures margin. August revenue NT$514.806bn brought Jan–Aug revenue to NT$3.387tn, but monthly revenue is unaudited and AI/customer mix is not fully disclosed. The ordinary moat is viable without incremental internal AI, though current demand and margins include AI accelerators.

## Data rights

| Input | Owner/exclusivity | Inference | Training/reuse | Labels/replication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Process-control/yield data | TSMC generated, product/customer-linked | Internal fab optimization | Cross-product reuse subject to customer confidentiality | Dense defects/yield labels; uniquely large scale |
| Customer designs/masks | Customer IP | Manufacturing-specific analysis under foundry contract | General training likely restricted; terms confidential | Highly relevant but not TSMC-owned |
| Equipment telemetry | Tool vendor/TSMC contract | Maintenance/process control | Cross-tool/fab rights contract-bound | Frequent labels; ASML/Applied/KLA may capture learning |
| Supplier/material lots | Suppliers plus TSMC QC | Lot-specific prediction | Pooled use terms unknown | Traceability useful; supplier dependence |

The strongest moat is cumulative process execution, not a universal right to train on customer designs. Tool vendors retain proprietary telemetry and may capture gains through service pricing.

## Mechanisms/competition

1. **Yield/process-window optimization (classical/deep ML).** Process/inspection data → parameter recommendations → more good dies per wafer at equal reliability → lower scrap/higher capacity → foundry margin. TSMC’s annual report says enterprise AI drives internal productivity, but provides no matched yield delta: E1/E2 programme only.
2. **Predictive maintenance/scheduling.** Tool telemetry and work-in-process → failure/dispatch prediction → uptime/cycle-time improvement → more wafer output. Existing advanced process control is already in baseline. No AI-specific downtime/cash reconciliation is disclosed.
3. **AI infrastructure demand.** AI accelerator demand raises advanced-node/packaging volume and pricing. This is a demand mechanism, not incumbent operating productivity; it must be assessed separately. Customers and equipment suppliers capture much of the ecosystem value.

Samsung and Intel challenge foundry capacity; ASML/KLA/Applied supply critical models and equipment. Customer chiplets and alternative accelerators may shift mix. Export controls or concentration can strand capex.

Q2 operating profit proxy is NT$1.27tn×60.3%=**NT$765.8bn**; annualized 5% scale hurdle **NT$153.2bn/year**. Illustrative cost bridge: NT$2.0tn eligible annual manufacturing cost ×5% incremental efficiency×50% realization×60% retained −NT$20bn AI cost = **NT$10bn/year**. Capacity path: NT$5tn eligible annual revenue×1% extra good-die output×65% contribution×70% retained = NT$22.8bn. Total **NT$32.8bn**, far below hurdle. Required eligible-output lift holding cost path is `(153.2-10)/(5000×.65×.7)=6.3%`. Inputs assumed; current deployed optimization remains baseline.

## Hypotheses/falsifiers/gates

Positive: unmatched process scale and labels let TSMC improve yield/uplift across immense capacity. Negative: semiconductor physics/equipment and capital are bottlenecks, suppliers own key data, and AI demand rather than internal productivity explains economics.

Falsifiers: matched node/product yields do not outperform prior learning curves; downtime/cycle time stays unchanged after deployment; recurring tool/compute cost absorbs savings. Questions: AI-controlled steps, incremental versus existing APC, customer design rights, supplier capture and offshore fab comparability.

Confidence mechanism high, deployment medium-low, capture medium. G0/G1 provisional pass; G2 insufficient because no scoped E2 outcome or controlled result. G3/G4 deferred.
