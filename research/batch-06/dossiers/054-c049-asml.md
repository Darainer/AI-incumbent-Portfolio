# ASML — evidence packet

**Identity.** ASML Holding N.V. ordinary shares, Euronext Amsterdam/Nasdaq:ASML, EUR reporting and primary quote. Cutoff/retrieval 12 September 2026; latest Q2 2026 released 15 July. Unreviewed; G3/G4 deferred. ASML embeds ML in computational lithography, metrology and inspection, a production product mechanism. Public sources do not isolate incremental wafer yield, service contribution or cash from AI versus physical-model software and hardware generations.

## Business/financial resilience

ASML sells DUV, EUV and High-NA EUV lithography systems plus installed-base management, upgrades and computational-lithography/metrology software. Its near-monopoly in EUV rests on decades of R&D, Zeiss optics, supplier networks, IP, customer co-development and service. Customers are a concentrated set of leading chipmakers. Export controls, customer capex cycles, supply constraints and geopolitical policy are central risks; Canon/Nikon compete in older lithography, and alternative patterning is a substitute at cost.

Q2 2026 net sales were **€9.326bn**, gross profit **€5.035bn**, gross margin **54.0%**, net income **€2.918bn**, basic EPS €7.59, and quarter-end cash plus short-term investments **€7.582bn**. [Q2 results](https://www.asml.com/en/news/press-releases/2026/q2-2026-financial-results), 15 July 2026. Management raised 2026 sales guidance to €43–45bn and gross margin 54–56%, driven by customer capacity plans including AI-related demand. Net bookings/backlog can be volatile and cancellation/timing matter. Hardware demand from AI data centers is separate from AI improving ASML products or operations.

## Mechanisms/challengers

1. **Computational lithography/process correction (physics + ML).** Mask/process/metrology context → predicted printing error and correction → wider process window/higher yield → software/system value and service revenue. ASML says ML is embedded in computational lithography and metrology/inspection. That establishes E2 deployed capability, not incremental AI-only economics.
2. **Inspection/metrology classification (deep ML).** Wafer images → defect classification/root-cause prioritization → fewer false alarms and faster ramp → customer yield and ASML software attach. KLA is a strong incumbent challenger. No controlled production KPI or ASML retained share is public.
3. **Predictive service.** Fleet telemetry → maintenance prediction → uptime and service efficiency → installed-base contribution. Customer data rights, rare failures and parts logistics constrain improvement; existing algorithms are already baseline.

KLA challenges inspection/process control; Synopsys and Siemens EDA plus customer in-house computational flows substitute at interfaces. Generic models are less useful without physical calibration, yet suppliers and customers may capture gains. Export limits can cap distribution.

## Hypotheses/falsifiers/gates

Positive: unique physics, tools and fleet feedback make ML inseparable from lithography value and allow capture through system/service pricing. Negative: customers own key data, physical hardware drives results, KLA/EDA rivals capture adjacent value and no incremental AI economics can be isolated.

Falsifiers: ML releases do not improve matched yield/tool-hour; paid software/service attach fails to rise after feature rollouts; customer restrictions prevent cross-fleet learning. Questions: rights, installed feature penetration, controlled yield/uptime, incremental compute/support cost and export impact.

Confidence mechanism high, deployment medium-high, capture medium. G0/G1 provisional pass; G2 provisional because E2 product use has a credible test route but no controlled/result cash evidence. G3/G4 deferred.
