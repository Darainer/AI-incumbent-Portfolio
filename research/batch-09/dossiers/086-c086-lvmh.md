## Provisional conclusion

LVMH has a durable conventional franchise based on scarce maisons, creative archives, craftsmanship, retail network and client relationships. The public evidence supports specific AI capabilities and, in some cases, management-reported operational use. It does not provide a controlled, quality-adjusted company result tied to retained incremental cash. The central underwriting issue is capture: operational improvement may pass to customers, be absorbed by implementation, already sit in the reported baseline, or be dominated by cycle and mix. The dossier therefore keeps G2 provisional and treats E3/E4 as unestablished.

## Conventional business and financial anchor

LVMH reports Fashion & Leather Goods, Selective Retailing, Wines & Spirits, Perfumes & Cosmetics, Watches & Jewelry and Other. Customer value arises from dependable execution, integration and domain expertise, not data volume alone. Competitive advantage must survive Kering and digital-native premium brands and general commerce/creative platforms. Switching costs and distribution help, but contracts, interoperability and procurement can reallocate gains.

For FY2025, company reporting identifies **€17.8bn profit from recurring operations**. This pre-tax recurring-profit measure governs the hurdle throughout the packet. Store investment, inventory, leases, currency, pricing and regional demand remain material confounders.

The [H1 2026 issuer release](https://www.lvmh.com/en/publications/accelerating-growth-in-the-second-quarter-solid-first-half-results) reports profit from recurring operations of €8.7bn and a 22.5% margin. LVMH and Google Cloud describe a strategic AI/cloud partnership, an E1 capability and rollout claim; no controlled maison-level sell-through or retained-cash result was disclosed. Luxury demand, price increases and China/aspirational-consumer mix remain dominant confounders.

The ordinary baseline already contains deployed analytics, automation and machine learning. Capital allocation must fund maintenance, growth investment, obligations and shareholder distributions before any incremental AI benefit. Financial scale permits experimentation, but it does not demonstrate quality, adoption or capture.

## Measurement contract and retained economics

Pre-register **full-price sell-through, inventory turns, repeat purchase at fixed cohort, conversion and contribution after creative/model cost**. Compare randomized or carefully matched sites, clients, products or cohorts against the current production system, including existing ML. Define the eligible workload before rollout; hold service, safety, accuracy, risk and customer-mix quality constant. Report sample size, duration, confidence interval, adoption, exception/rework rate and all human/model/implementation cost. The known public result is insufficient for E3. A future annual report, product cohort study or customer renewal disclosure linking the KPI to contribution could close the gap.

The framework's five-year research convention is **€890m** for this packet. 5%×€17.8bn FY2025 profit from recurring operations=€890m. A €5bn eligible pool×8%×50%×50%−€80m=€20m; brand demand and pricing dominate. Customer-facing novelty without incremental contribution is not capture. Assumption inputs are illustrative and reproducible. They are neither guidance nor valuation. Released time counts only if removed or redeployed to valued output; the same capacity cannot be counted as both cost saving and revenue. Capital, working capital and tax would need separate treatment.

## Competing hypotheses, falsifiers and gates

**Strongest positive hypothesis:** proprietary workflow context and distribution let LVMH deploy validated AI faster than standalone vendors, with better feedback and a retained service, pricing, throughput or capital benefit. **Strongest negative hypothesis:** model capability commoditizes, customer rights prevent pooling, implementation and verification absorb gains, and Kering or digital-native premium brands and general commerce/creative platforms forces pass-through.

Three falsifiers are: (1) matched deployments fail to improve full-price sell-through, inventory turns, repeat purchase at fixed cohort, conversion and contribution after creative/model cost at equal quality; (2) five-year retained benefit after recurring AI and implementation cost remains below the stated 5% hurdle; (3) contracts, portability or competitor performance show that the alleged data/workflow advantage is readily replicated. Unresolved questions are eligible-workload share, exact production penetration, customer-level retention/pricing, recurring model cost, contract training rights and the latest post-FY2025 adverse development.

G0 passes because asset, mechanism, capture route and falsifiers are explicit. G1 is provisionally supported by the conventional franchise and financial capacity, subject to latest-period refresh and sector risks. G2 remains **open**: scoped E1/E2 can support further testing, but E3/E4 are absent. G3 and G4 are **deferred by user**. Confidence is **medium** for mechanism, **low-to-medium** for deployment breadth and **low** for capture.
