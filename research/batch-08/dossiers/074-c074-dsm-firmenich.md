# dsm-firmenich — evidence packet

**Identity and scope.** dsm-firmenich AG, SIX:DSFIR and Euronext Amsterdam:DSFIR, EUR reporting currency; cutoff 12 September 2026. This packet treats AI as a set of narrow mechanisms in formulation, sensory prediction, scientific search and employee workflow. It does not treat the broad label “digital” as evidence. The latest reported period is H1 2026; FY2025 remains the annual materiality anchor. The company describes deployed AI tools, but does not disclose controlled productivity, product revenue attributable to AI or retained cash. G3/G4 are deferred.

## Business and financial baseline

dsm-firmenich sells fragrances, flavors, beauty ingredients, nutrition and health ingredients. Its defensible assets are application laboratories, proprietary formulas, sensory panels, regulatory files, customer briefs and long supplier/customer relationships. These assets create useful training data, although rights can be limited by customer confidentiality and licensed scientific content. Givaudan and Symrise have similar domain data and formulation capabilities, while specialist software and generative-chemistry firms can supply tools.

H1 2026 continuing sales were **€4.664bn**, adjusted EBITDA **€900m**, adjusted EBIT **€435m**, net profit **€225m**, adjusted gross operating FCF **€320m** and net debt **€4.424bn**. Management attributed growth to volumes, synergies and win rates and separately targeted €100m restructuring savings, so none is assigned to AI. [H1 2026 release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/30/3335753/0/en/dsm-firmenich-reports-h1-2026-results.html), 30 July 2026. For 2025 continuing operations, sales were **€9.034bn**, adjusted EBITDA **€1.772bn**, adjusted operating profit **€861m**, reported operating profit **€711m**, and adjusted gross operating free cash flow **€950m**. Total-group sales were €12.521bn and adjusted EBITDA €2.279bn, but reported operating loss was €877m because Animal Nutrition & Health was classified as discontinued and portfolio charges distorted comparison. [2025 financial results](https://annualreport.dsm-firmenich.com/2025/our-performance/financial-performance/financial-results.html). Continuing-operation operating profit is therefore the cleaner materiality anchor. Portfolio separation, pricing, currency and input costs must remain outside any AI bridge.

## Mechanisms, evidence and ownership

The 2025 report says the company deployed an AI formula-generation assistant, AI-powered sensory descriptions, tools for product matching, formulation and evidence generation, and Copilot Chat for all employees. [Data governance and AI](https://annualreport.dsm-firmenich.com/2025/our-performance/data-governance-and-ai.html). That establishes E2 product/workflow deployment by company report. It does not establish incremental revenue, fewer lab cycles, or labor removal.

**Formulation candidate ranking.** Inputs are customer constraints, ingredient functions, historic formulas, panel scores and stability results. A model proposes or ranks candidates; chemists test them; the economic output is fewer physical iterations and faster customer sampling at equal acceptance and margin. Qualification, regulation, ingredient availability and sensory judgment cap automation. The company says bioinformatics, chemoinformatics and generative/agentic AI can raise discovery hit rates and reduce experimental cycles. [Science approach](https://annualreport.dsm-firmenich.com/2025/our-company/science-research/our-approach.html). This is a mechanism claim, not a prospective cohort result.

**Sensory description and product matching.** Models translate formula features, panel language and briefs into candidate descriptions or matched products. Value could come from faster brief response and higher win rates. A reliable test must compare contemporaneous briefs randomized between existing search and AI-assisted workflows, with the same commercial teams and customer mix. Public disclosure supplies no such result.

**Evidence generation and knowledge search.** Retrieval and generation can shorten scientific literature review and regulatory drafting. Human verification remains necessary because an unsupported health or safety statement has asymmetric downside. The relevant KPI is accepted dossier hours per submission, with correction and regulator-query rates held constant—not prompts, licenses or documents generated.

**Employee copilots.** Availability to all employees is evidence of access, not adoption or savings. Any benefit should be measured through completed processes per paid hour, after review time, license/compute expense and displaced vendor spend.

Rights are strongest for experiments performed and formulas owned by dsm-firmenich. Customer briefs and co-developed formulas can be contract-restricted. Panel data require consent and governance; published science can carry database or publisher restrictions. Supplier specifications may be licensed. The report describes a Responsible AI committee and data standards, useful controls but not proof of unrestricted reuse. Models or cloud providers may capture part of the economics through license and inference fees. Customers can capture faster development through lower prices or shorter lead times.

The strongest negative evidence is absence: no disclosed randomized brief win rate, laboratory cycle reduction, qualified-product count per R&D euro, recurring AI P&L, or customer retention differential. Competitor tools show the mechanism is reproducible. Portfolio changes can swamp group trends. A retrospective association between tool use and faster projects would remain exposed to selection bias; only prospective matched cohorts would support a productivity claim.

## KPI contract and materiality

The primary contract is **median lab iterations per accepted customer brief**, AI-assisted versus prospectively matched conventional briefs, controlling for category, region, novelty and team; report acceptance, gross margin and complaint/rework rates. Secondary KPIs are time from brief to accepted sample, commercial win rate, qualified discoveries per R&D euro, and regulatory drafting hours with correction/query rates. Report eligible briefs, assignment method, adoption, confidence intervals, human review and total recurring tool cost.

Five percent of 2025 continuing reported operating profit is **€35.55m/year** (`€711m×5%`). An illustrative bridge assumes 20,000 eligible annual briefs, €18,000 avoidable lab/commercial cost per brief, 15% cycle reduction, 55% realization and 70% retained benefit: **€20.79m**. Add €1.5bn of specialty revenue exposed to matching, 0.5% incremental revenue, 35% contribution margin and 70% retention: **€1.84m**. Less €12m recurring data/model/control expense gives **€10.63m/year**, below the hurdle. Required cycle reduction holding other assumptions is `(35.55+12−1.84)/(20,000×18,000×.55×.70)=33.0%`. All inputs are scenarios, not guidance. Because the anchor is pre-tax operating profit, the bridge is also pre-tax. Adjusted EBITDA or cash flow must not be mixed into this comparison.

Positive case: dense proprietary experiment and sensory data can reduce iteration in high-value, repeat workflows. Negative case: data rights fragment by customer, competitors possess similar corpora, physical testing stays binding, and savings may show up as faster service rather than retained margin. Falsifiers are no prospective reduction in cycles, lower acceptance or margin, flat qualified output per R&D euro, and recurring cost exceeding realized savings. Open questions are eligible-brief volume, active rather than licensed users, cross-customer training rights, model-vendor economics and the portfolio-adjusted baseline.

Confidence: mechanism medium-high; deployment medium; capture low. G0 pass; G1 provisional given portfolio transition; G2 insufficient because public evidence is E2 deployment without controlled or cash outcomes. G3/G4 deferred.
