# C028 — Adyen evidence dossier

**Unreviewed agent work.** Adyen N.V.; ordinary shares ADYEN on Euronext Amsterdam, EUR quote/reporting currency. Cutoff/retrieval 12 September 2026. Latest period H1 ended 30 June 2026, published 13 August 2026; FY2025 latest audited.

## Business and financial anchor

Adyen provides acquiring, processing, payment methods, risk management, issuing and financial products through one global platform. It earns net revenue after pass-through interchange and scheme fees, so processed volume is not revenue. The potential scarce asset is the unified, real-time payment stream across online and physical channels, linked to merchant outcomes. AI may improve authorization, fraud and personalization, but merchants, networks and issuers own much context and can multi-home.

[H1 2026 results](https://www.adyen.com/press-and-media/adyen-publishes-h1-2026-financial-results-3wjne) reported processed volume **€803.8bn**, up 24%; net revenue about **€1.3bn**, up 19% reported/21% constant currency; EBITDA **€641.5m**, 49% margin (50% excluding one-time transaction cost); free-cash-flow conversion 86%; and capex equal to 5% of net revenue. FCF is company-defined as EBITDA less capex and lease payments, so it differs from statutory cash flow. Cash generation and a historically net-cash balance support investment, while H1 acquisitions of Talon.One and Orb add integration and one-time-cost confounders.

FY2025 annual report was published 5 March 2026. H2 2025 net revenue was €1.27bn and full-year EBITDA margin about 53%; volume growth lagged expectations. H1 guidance was raised to 21–23% constant-currency net-revenue growth for 2026 and >55% EBITDA margin by 2028. Guidance and share reactions are not evidence of AI value. Conventional advantages are a single code base, direct acquiring licenses, enterprise integrations and global/local payment coverage. Competition from Stripe, PayPal/Braintree, Checkout.com, Worldpay and local acquirers constrains take rate.

The [privacy statement](https://www.adyen.com/policies-and-disclaimer/privacy-policy) and merchant contracts govern controller/processor roles. They do not grant a universal right to train across merchants. PCI/tokenization security supports trust but is not data exclusivity.

## Mechanisms, challenge and evidence

1. **Authorization optimization (classical ML).** Unified transaction history → select routing, retry and identity signals → more legitimate approvals at fixed fraud/fees → more merchant sales and Adyen volume/net revenue. Adyen reports Uplift and Dynamic Identification raised customer conversion by **0.9 percentage points on average** by H1 end. This is E2 management-reported production and approaches E3 only if the underlying matched design, fraud and fee controls are disclosed; they were not.
2. **Risk management (classical ML).** Network signals/chargebacks → score and block fraud → lower loss/chargebacks at equal legitimate acceptance → retention, pricing and avoided risk. RevenueProtect is deployed, but no current controlled loss-and-approval cohort was found. E2 product deployment, E1 effectiveness.
3. **Agentic commerce (agentic execution).** Merchant catalog/consumer mandate/token → let AI agents initiate authorized purchase → incremental processed volume/contribution after fraud, protocol and model costs. Adyen Agentic launched in H1 2026. It is E1 availability; transaction adoption, consent disputes, fraud and net economics are unknown.

**Stripe** is the direct challenger with global acquiring, Radar and agentic-commerce tooling. **Visa/Mastercard and issuer networks** are substitutes/suppliers: they see broader network data and can mandate token/protocol rules, capturing economics. Large merchants can route among processors. More approvals may lower take rate through competition; personalization benefit may accrue mainly to merchants.

Five percent of H1 EBITDA annualized only as a rough scale (`2 × €641.5m`) is **€64.15m annual pre-tax**; seasonality makes this a proxy. Illustration: 0.9ppt incremental approval on assumed €200bn eligible legitimate attempts × assumed 0.20% Adyen net take rate × 40% contribution × 50% retention less €20m run cost = **negative €19.28m**. Every input except the 0.9ppt claim is assumed, and uplift may displace retries rather than create volume. The calculation shows this particular bridge does not clear the hurdle.

Positive: unified enterprise payment data creates measurable, rapid feedback and merchant value, with volume-based capture. Negative: network/issuer data is broader, rights are constrained, improvements are competed into price, and agentic protocols shift interface power to models.

Falsifiers: conversion lift vanishes after fraud/fee control; merchants multi-route and take rate falls faster than volume benefit; agentic disputes/fraud prevent scale. Confidence mechanism **medium-high**, deployment **medium**, capture **low-medium**. G0/G1 pass provisionally; G2 provisional because E2 exists but controlled quality/cash retention is absent.
