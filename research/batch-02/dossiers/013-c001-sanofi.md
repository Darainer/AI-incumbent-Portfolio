# Sanofi — evidence packet

**Identity and boundary.** Sanofi SA ordinary shares trade on Euronext Paris (SAN; EUR); this packet concerns the French parent, which reports in euros, rather than the US ADR. Research cutoff and retrieval date: **12 September 2026**. Latest financial period reviewed: H1 2026, published 30 July 2026. Status: unreviewed evidence packet; G3/G4 deferred by user. The short conclusion is deliberately conditional: Sanofi has deployable scientific data and meaningful AI programmes, but public evidence reaches production tools and preclinical milestones, not prospective proof that AI has improved stage-adjusted clinical productivity or shareholder cash.

## Conventional business and resilience

Sanofi is now principally a biopharmaceutical and vaccines company after the Opella consumer-health separation. Growth is concentrated in Dupixent, partnered with Regeneron, plus immunology, rare disease, neurology and vaccines. That franchise provides cash and global clinical/commercial infrastructure, but concentration and the 2031 Dupixent patent horizon make pipeline output unusually important. The 2025 Blueprint Medicines acquisition added Ayvakit and mast-cell programmes but also acquisition cost and integration risk; it confounds simple comparisons of 2026 growth and pipeline productivity.

## Scarce assets, rights and replication

| Input | Owner / exclusivity | Permitted customer-specific inference | Training / pooled reuse | Labels and replication threat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sanofi assays, molecules and failed experiments | Sanofi-created; compound structures and some know-how proprietary | Internal research appears permissible | Internal reuse plausible; exact governance undisclosed | Experimental outcomes are useful labels, but rivals have comparable labs and public biology |
| Clinical-trial records and biomarker samples | Mixed rights: sponsor, sites and patients; consent/protocol bound | Protocol-approved analysis only | Cross-trial model training not established publicly | High-value longitudinal outcomes; privacy, consent and cohort shift constrain use |
| Formation Bio/OpenAI collaboration data | Each party retains its assets; commercial terms undisclosed | Collaboration states customized models/software | No public evidence that Sanofi may train across Formation Bio trials or OpenAI customers | Engineering is replicable; proprietary trial operations/context may differentiate |
| Exscientia/Recursion programme results | Contractual joint work; ownership by programme terms not public | Programme-specific use established | Cross-programme reuse unknown | Two discovery milestones are labels, but clinical validity remains unobserved |

Possession therefore does not establish an unrestricted data moat. Sanofi’s stronger advantage is the combination of wet-lab validation, disease expertise and the ability to sponsor trials. OpenAI and specialist biotech suppliers retain bargaining power; AstraZeneca’s acquisition of Modella AI and Takeda’s Iambic alliance show credible alternative access to similar tooling.

## Mechanisms and challengers

1. **Multimodal discovery (classical/deep ML).** Proprietary assay and molecule data feed models that propose/select compounds; Sanofi and Exscientia reported two programme milestones in October 2024. The quality-adjusted output would be candidates with better potency, selectivity and developability, ultimately raising stage-adjusted pipeline value. Today this is E2 for an active partnered workflow and E1 for clinical economics. A milestone is not prospective clinical success.
2. **Trial recruitment and protocol operations (generative/agentic assistance).** Sanofi, Formation Bio and OpenAI announced customized software, and Formation Bio introduced Muse for patient recruitment. The route is protocol/site/patient context → identification and outreach support → shorter enrollment at equal representation and safety → lower time-to-readout → retained R&D economics. Public sources do not disclose randomized comparisons, eligible trial share, false-match rates, adoption, or net cost.
3. **Enterprise research assistance.** Internal assistants can retrieve governed knowledge and draft work, potentially releasing scientist or regulatory time. Generic enterprise models and CRO software are substitutes, and human verification limits conversion. No end-to-end cash reconciliation was located.

The incumbent pharma challenger is Roche, which combines pharma and diagnostics data and in 2026 deployed a large NVIDIA AI computing estate. Entrants include Formation Bio/Recursion and model vendors. They can capture economics through fees, milestones or platform dependency, while CROs and hospitals control operational data.

## KPI contract and materiality

| KPI | Baseline / comparator | Eligible workload | Quality condition | Known result | Closing event |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Months and external spend to recruit | Similar prior/concurrent non-Muse trial | Muse-enabled protocols/sites | Enrollment diversity, eligibility accuracy, protocol deviations no worse | Undisclosed | Completed trial report plus cost bridge |
| Stage-adjusted NPV per R&D euro | Conventional portfolio on same assumptions | AI-attributed programmes | No double-counting acquired assets | Undisclosed | Annual pipeline cohort disclosure |

The 5% research convention equals **€607m**, using FY2025 BOI of €12.149bn as a reported-profit scale proxy. For pharma, this is not a near-term R&D cut target. Illustrative prospective bridge: 20 AI-influenced development entries × €250m risk-adjusted value at entry × a 15% improvement in quality-adjusted transition value × 80% Sanofi ownership/capture − €100m annualized platform/validation cost = **€500m**, below the hurdle. Break-even requires 17.7% improvement: `(€607m + €100m)/(20×€250m×80%)`. Every input except reported BOI is an assumption. Discovery speed without better clinical transition value fails the test.

## Hypotheses, falsifiers and gate view

The strongest positive hypothesis is that Sanofi’s immunology biology, experimental feedback and trial network let it kill weak projects earlier and direct capital to better candidates. The strongest negative hypothesis is that publicized tools accelerate drafting and candidate generation while clinical biology, recruiting, safety and payer value remain binding; suppliers and acquired biotechs capture much of any gain.

Falsifiers: (1) AI-attributed cohorts do not improve matched transition rates or time-to-kill by 2030; (2) Sanofi discontinues or repeatedly replaces major AI partnerships without production metrics; (3) net platform/validation cost rises while risk-adjusted pipeline output per R&D euro remains flat. Unresolved: Which programmes are materially AI-selected? What consents permit cross-trial learning? What is Muse’s production trial count and matched enrollment result? How are supplier economics structured?

Confidence: mechanism **medium**; deployment **low-medium**; capture **low**. G0 passes. G1 is provisionally supported by a resilient core and identifiable assets, subject to rights detail. G2 is **provisional/insufficient**: E2 workflow evidence exists, but a credible route to prospective controlled clinical-productivity proof is not yet populated with results. G3/G4 deferred.
