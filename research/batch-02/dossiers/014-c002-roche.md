# Roche — evidence packet

**Identity and boundary.** Roche Holding AG has voting bearer shares (RO) and non-voting equity securities/genussscheine (ROG) on SIX, quoted in CHF; this packet addresses the group and treats ROG as the commonly traded security reference without valuation. Reporting currency: CHF. Cutoff/retrieval: **12 September 2026**. Latest financial period: H1 2026, published 23 July 2026. Unreviewed; G3/G4 deferred. Roche has a rare combined pharma/diagnostics asset and real AI infrastructure and pathology deployments, yet disclosed evidence still does not show that these tools prospectively raise clinical success or generate retained incremental cash.

## Conventional business and financial anchor

Roche comprises Pharmaceuticals and Diagnostics. Pharma’s marketed portfolio includes Ocrevus, Hemlibra, Vabysmo, Phesgo and Xolair; Diagnostics spans core lab, molecular, pathology and point-of-care systems. The combination can connect biomarker discovery, companion-diagnostic development, clinical trials and installed laboratory workflows. Economics nevertheless depend on drug exclusivity, pipeline outcomes, reimbursement and instrument/reagent pull-through. China pricing reform pressures diagnostics, while biosimilars erode mature drugs.

Roche’s moat is validation, regulated distribution and the ability to pair diagnostics with therapy development. It is not a demonstrated exclusive ownership of patient records. PathAI customers’ and data contributors’ pre-existing rights remain contract dependent after acquisition.

## Mechanisms, deployment and competitive pressure

1. **Digital pathology workflow (deep ML/generative assistance).** Slides plus diagnostic context feed image management and algorithms that prioritize, quantify or support companion-diagnostic calls. PathAI’s system was already partnered with Roche from 2021 and expanded in 2024; the May 2026 acquisition agreement would integrate it into Diagnostics. Output must be faster case completion at non-inferior accuracy and lower repeat/manual work, producing software/reagent revenue and lab retention. The announcement is E2 for an existing partnership/platform, not E3 efficacy or E4 cash.
2. **Discovery and development modeling (foundation models/AI infrastructure).** Roche and NVIDIA expanded compute to 2,176 Blackwell GPUs across US and Europe in March 2026. The intended tasks include modeling, data analysis and trials. Compute in service does not prove better candidates; prospective cohort transition rates remain undisclosed. The economic line is stage-adjusted pipeline value after compute and validation cost.
3. **Biomarker and companion-diagnostic development.** Combining therapy cohorts with tissue/assay insight could improve patient selection and speed algorithm development. Retention could occur in drug success, companion-test revenue and installed-base pull-through, but transfer pricing between divisions, clinical endpoints and payer capture obscure attribution.

Paige is a credible pathology entrant: it offers regulated applications and released PRISM/Virchow foundation-model work; the 2024 PRISM paper reports benchmark capability, illustrating that model representations may diffuse. AstraZeneca/Modella is a pharma challenger and NVIDIA remains a supplier with bargaining power. Laboratories may capture time savings, and open-source feature extractors can lower switching barriers.

| KPI / unit | Baseline/comparator | Eligible workload | Quality condition | Known result | Closure |

| Phase transition and time-to-kill | Pre-2023 matched Genentech/Roche programmes | Materially AI-selected cohort | Same modality/area/risk; safety intact | Not disclosed | Five-year entry-cohort reconciliation |

The 5% convention equals **CHF595m for the six-month period** (CHF11.9bn × 5%) or CHF1.19bn per year if H1 is mechanically annualized solely as a scale proxy. A recurring pathology bridge uses consistent annual units: 20,000 labs/care sites × CHF100k eligible annual software/service value × 40% adoption × 45% contribution margin × 70% Roche retention − CHF100m recurring compute/support = **CHF152m per year**, only 13% of the annualized hurdle. Stage-adjusted R&D programme value is a one-time present value and is therefore reported separately: 15 AI-influenced entries × CHF250m stage-adjusted value × 15% quality lift × 80% retention = **CHF450m one-time present value**. It cannot fill an annual CHF1.038bn “gap” without explicit cohort timing, tax and annuitization. All operating inputs are assumptions; no published result establishes either bridge.

## Competing hypotheses, falsifiers and gates

Positive: Roche can use diagnostics labels and pharma validation to create a closed discovery–biomarker–test loop that pure pharma or software rivals cannot easily reproduce, then distribute improvements through regulated systems. Negative: patient/lab rights fragment the data, academic and commercial foundation models commoditize representation, and physical trials remain the bottleneck; Roche pays vendors/acquisitions while customers capture workflow savings.

Falsifiers: (1) PathAI integration fails to produce measurable multisite throughput at non-inferior diagnostic quality and paid renewals; (2) post-2023 AI-attributed drug cohorts show no matched improvement in time-to-kill or transition value; (3) compute/support and acquisition amortization exceed incremental diagnostics contribution for three years. Unresolved: Was PathAI closed by cutoff and what data licenses transfer? What production sites use AI algorithms? What are cleared-case penetration and attach revenue? Which discovery programmes were materially AI-selected?

Confidence: mechanism **medium-high**; deployment **medium**; capture **low-medium**. G0 passes. G1 provisionally passes, with rights unresolved. G2 is **insufficient/provisional**: Roche has E2 infrastructure and partnership evidence, but the E3 pathology benchmark belongs to Paige/academic authors and cannot support Roche's gate. Roche has not disclosed quality-adjusted production or retained economics. G3/G4 deferred.
