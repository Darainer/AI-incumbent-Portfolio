**Status:** unreviewed research packet. **Research cutoff:** 12 September 2026. **Latest financial period:** Q3 FY2026 (ended 2 August 2026); published 20 August 2026. **Security:** Deere, DE, NYSE; quote currency USD, reporting currency USD. ## Provisional conclusion and conventional business

Deere has a credible incumbent-AI mechanism because it controls installed connected machines, dealer service history and agronomic Operations Center context. The current record supports capabilities and some reported production deployment, generally E1–E2. It does **not** establish controlled quality-adjusted improvement across a representative workload or incremental cash retained by shareholders. The investment question therefore remains operational: can management disclose a stable baseline, eligible workload, quality guardrail, realized economics and recurring technology cost?

The latest conventional anchor is [Q3 FY2026 (ended 2 August 2026)](https://www.deere.com/en/our-company/news-and-announcements/news-releases/2026/corporate/deere-reports-third-quarter-net-income-of-1-379-billion/): **$12.608bn net sales and revenues** and **$1.379bn net income; equipment operations margin 14.4%**. These figures are reported unless explicitly labelled adjusted. FY2025 operating cash flow and financing receivables remain the relevant cycle baseline; current-quarter FCF was not isolated in the opened release. Manufacturing debt must be separated from John Deere Financial borrowings; the finance arm funds dealer/customer credit. The principal confounders are cycle, mix, pricing, acquisitions/restructuring, working capital and capital intensity. Existing prediction, optimization and automation are already in these reported results; only incremental improvement from here belongs in an AI bridge.

The core business earns from its established products, services, distribution and contracts. Customers buy an economic outcome rather than “AI”: availability, speed, accuracy, convenience, risk transfer or regulated service. Competition therefore determines whether lower task cost becomes higher margin, lower price, better service, employee output, supplier payments or customer surplus. Capital allocation must fund the installed business first. Any AI claim that ignores maintenance capital, implementation, cloud/model cost, verification labor or cannibalization is incomplete.

## Scarce assets and data rights

| Input | Owner / exclusivity | Customer-specific inference | Training / cross-customer reuse | Labels and replication threat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| installed connected machines, dealer service history and agronomic Operations Center context | Company IP, physical/distribution rights and customer data have different owners; exclusivity is partial | Contract and law may allow processing to deliver the service | **Unknown unless the contract says so.** Possession and privacy controls do not create a pooled-training right | Operational outcomes can be frequent, but selection, drift and missing counterfactuals remain; incumbent rivals can collect similar data |
| Customer records and prompts | Normally customer/data-subject controlled | Generally limited to contracted purpose and authorized users | Opened [Deere privacy/terms material](https://www.deere.com/en/privacy-and-data/) did not establish universal training or cross-customer reuse | Good local context; portability, deletion, confidentiality and regulation constrain compounding |

The defensible asset is more often integration, trusted workflow and execution permission than a proprietary foundation model. The dossier found no opened contract proving unrestricted pooled learning. That gap matters: a generic model can use retrieved customer context without giving the vendor an exclusive data flywheel. Rights also vary by geography, product and customer configuration.

## AI mechanisms and competitive challenge

**Mechanism 1 — scoped workflow improvement.** See & Spray computer vision identifies weeds and controls individual nozzles; customers reported nearly 50% lower non-residual herbicide use in 2025, but crop, field and chemistry mix are uncontrolled. The chain is asset/context → task prediction or generation → deployed human/system workflow → quality-adjusted completed output → cost, revenue or risk line → retained contribution after implementation and sharing. This is E1 where only capability is documented and E2 where management or a named user reports production. No available disclosure provides a controlled matched cohort plus full cost.

**Mechanism 2 — execution or adjacent demand.** autonomy and predictive service can convert machine/field telemetry into fewer operator hours and less downtime; availability is model-, crop- and geography-specific. Agentic execution deserves a higher bar than assistance: permission boundaries, rollback, audit logs, exception rates and human escalation must be measured. “Hours saved” is capacity, not cash, until staffing is removed or redeployed into valued output without deterioration.

**Challenger and substitute.** CNH Industrial/AGCO plus retrofit autonomy and vision suppliers can pair commodity models with farmer data. The primary challenger evidence is the rival’s own [product/technology material](https://www.cnh.com/en-US/innovation/Pages/precision-technology.aspx), which proves capability rather than superiority. A substitute can also bypass the incumbent interface, use open standards or customer-controlled data, and compete on price. Supplier capture is material where cloud, models, chips or implementation partners charge per use. Customer capture is material where contracts reprice savings or where competition passes them through.

**Adverse evidence.** Large-ag demand, crop economics and tariffs dominate earnings; Deere guided to a roughly $1.2bn FY2026 pre-tax tariff burden, so technology adoption cannot be inferred from consolidated margins. This is not proof that AI fails; it prevents attribution of group results to AI. It also highlights labor realization and capital intensity. Headcount changes, restructuring or growth cannot be counted as AI benefit without a task-level bridge.

## Measurement contract and materiality arithmetic

The framework’s research convention is **$250m, approximately 5% of the midpoint of FY2026 net-income guidance ($4.75–5.0bn)**. This is a scale test, not a forecast or valuation.

| KPI / unit | Baseline and comparator | Eligible workload | Quality condition | Known result / next proof event |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Completed task per paid hour | Pre-rollout production system, including existing ML; matched users/sites | Only tasks technically and legally eligible | Equal error, safety, customer outcome and rework | Current disclosure incomplete; require a 12-month matched cohort |
| Economic output per transaction/asset/account | Same mix, geography and risk cohort | Production volume actually exposed | Equal loss, availability, latency and complaints | Require cohort contribution after discounts, model and support cost |
| Deployment share | Named active production users ÷ eligible users/workloads | Exclude pilots, demos and unavailable geographies | Sustained use and renewal | Require active-use, renewal and rollback disclosure |
| Retained cash | Incremental contribution or removed cash cost | Avoid double counting released capacity | After labor realization, capex, cloud/model and implementation cost | Require finance reconciliation at annual/interim reporting |

Illustrative cost bridge: `$3.0bn eligible annual service, warranty, engineering and support cost × 15% task efficiency × 50% realization × 50% retained share − $75m recurring AI expense = $37.5m annual operating benefit`. Every input except the cited profit scale is an analyst assumption. The result is below the 5% hurdle, demonstrating that plausible task improvement may be immaterial after realization and recurring cost. Customer savings are not treated as company cost. Revenue mechanisms need a separate bridge using incremental paid volume or price, less cannibalization, rebates, servicing, bad debt where relevant and model cost. Physical/infrastructure mechanisms also require incremental capex and working capital.

## Hypotheses, falsifiers and evidence gates

**Strongest positive hypothesis:** the installed asset and distribution let Deere embed models in a difficult workflow, observe outcomes and retain part of better throughput, decisions or service. **Strongest negative hypothesis:** generic models and rivals reach the same customer context, contractual rights block pooled learning, and customers, workers or suppliers capture the gain while implementation/capital cost remains with the company.

Three falsifiers: (1) by the next two annual reporting cycles, management still cannot disclose active production penetration and a quality-adjusted KPI for a material workload; (2) renewals, usage or contribution margin deteriorate as AI pricing, cannibalization and supplier costs rise; (3) matched rollouts fail to beat the existing production system after errors, rework, safety/risk and implementation cost. Core-business deterioration must be diagnosed separately from AI failure.

Unresolved questions are the precise customer-data licenses; whether feedback may improve a pooled model; eligible workload; representative baseline; error and escalation rate; incremental price/volume; labor realization; recurring inference/support cost; required capex; and the share of savings retained after competition or regulation.

**G0:** provisionally satisfied: asset, mechanisms, capture hypothesis and falsifiers are explicit. **G1:** provisionally satisfied only subject to the stated balance-sheet, governance and rights gaps. **G2:** provisional **watch / incomplete**: E1–E2 supports a route to measurement, but E3 quality-adjusted evidence and E4 retained cash are absent. **G3/G4:** deferred by user. Confidence is **moderate** in mechanism, **low-to-moderate** in broad deployment and **low** in capture.
