# C026 Visa — evidence dossier

**Unreviewed agent packet. Research cutoff:** 12 September 2026. **Security:** Visa Inc. Class A common stock, NYSE: V; quote and reporting currency USD. **Latest financial period:** fiscal Q3 / nine months ended 30 June 2026, released 28 July 2026. No valuation, ranking or recommendation is made.

## Decision frame and ordinary business

Visa owns a globally distributed payment-network connection and sells authorization, clearing, settlement and value-added services; it does not issue cards or extend consumer credit. Its scarce economic asset is therefore the two-sided acceptance/issuer network, transaction context and trusted real-time workflow rather than loan receivables. Scale remains exceptional: FY2025 processed transactions were 257.5bn, payment volume $14.2tn and payment credentials 4.9bn. That scale supports availability, global acceptance and fraud pattern coverage, while client incentives, regulation and alternative rails constrain capture.

The conventional business is strong without incremental AI. FY2025 net revenue was $40.0bn; GAAP operating profit was $23.994bn (revenue less $16.006bn operating expense), net income $20.058bn and operating cash flow $23.059bn. Purchases of property, equipment and technology were $1.482bn, producing a simple $21.577bn operating-cash-flow less investment proxy; this is not management-defined free cash flow. Visa repurchased $18.316bn of Class A shares and paid $4.634bn of dividends. Cash and equivalents were $17.2bn plus $2.4bn available-for-sale debt securities at FY-end, while FY2025 included $3.924bn of senior-note issuance. The [2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316125000089/v-20250930.htm) also makes the economic boundary clear: issuers and acquirers remain clients and Visa receives net revenue after client incentives.

Q3 FY2026 net revenue was $11.6bn, GAAP net income $5.6bn and GAAP operating expense $4.8bn; 71.7bn transactions were processed, up 10%, and client incentives rose 18%. Cash, equivalents and investment securities were $13.9bn. The quarter contained $563m severance and $237m litigation provision, so reported profitability is a noisy scale anchor. Management also disclosed $6.2bn of quarterly repurchases and dividends. These figures and the 1.90bn diluted Class A share count come from the [opened SEC-filed earnings release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1403161/000140316126000103/q32026earningsrelease.htm). Growth can reflect volume, cross-border mix, pricing, acquisitions and currencies, not AI.

## AI mechanisms, challengers and negative evidence

**1. Authorization risk (classical/deep ML).** Network context feeds Visa Advanced Authorization / Deep Authorization; the score informs issuer approve/decline rules; quality-adjusted output is more legitimate approvals at a fixed fraud rate; Visa may capture service revenue and incremental processing. Visa says VAA evaluates up to 400 attributes and 8,000-plus banks use VAA/VRM. Its [Emirates NBD case study](https://corporate.visa.com/content/dam/VCOM/corporate/solutions/documents/emirates-nbd-case-study-final.pdf) attributes $105m of prevented losses across Visa and non-Visa portfolios in 2023. This is identified-customer production evidence (E2), not a controlled study: the denominator, counterfactual, implementation costs and false-decline effects are not disclosed.

**2. Account-to-account scam detection (classical ML with explanatory assistance).** Featurespace transfer learning plus participating-bank signals produces risk alerts and plain-language explanations. Visa reported a 75% increase in fraud detection in the first six months and more than 40% fewer unnecessary alerts for enhanced A2A Protect. The release does not publish sample size, comparator design or loss-value reconciliation, so it is management-reported E2, not E3. Strategically, this mechanism is also a hedge against the rail that could bypass card economics.

**3. Internal generative assistance.** Q3 severance and commentary about reshaping work may be consistent with automation, but no opened primary source causally allocates savings to AI. It stays E1/E0 and is excluded from ordinary earnings uplift.

Mastercard is the most direct incumbent challenger: its Decision Intelligence products use network information for fraud decisions, limiting exclusivity. Account-to-account payment schemes and real-time bank rails are the substitute: they can remove Visa's card transaction and fee, although Visa is attempting to sell protection across those rails. Negative evidence is the absence of disclosed controlled authorization tests and retained pricing. Client incentives rising faster than Q3 revenue illustrates bargaining and mix pressure; interchange litigation and fee regulation can transfer gains to merchants or issuers. Fraud adaptation may merely relocate losses.

## KPI and materiality contract

| KPI | Baseline / comparator | Eligible workload | Quality condition | Known result | Closing event |

| net Visa contribution per scored transaction | no-product client cohort | paid risk-service volume | after incentives, model and support cost | unknown | value-added-services revenue/attach disclosure |

Illustrative 5% hurdle uses FY2025 reported operating profit because a normalized measure is not disclosed: 5% × $23.994bn = **$1.200bn annual operating benefit**. A revenue bridge could be eligible scored volume × incremental fee/contribution, but neither unit price nor incremental margin is public. A cost bridge is also illustrative: if eligible annual operating cost were $4.0bn (assumption), task efficiency 20%, realization 60%, retained share 60%, and recurring AI expense $0.20bn, benefit = $4.0bn × 20% × 60% × 60% − $0.20bn = **$0.088bn**, far below the hurdle. The more plausible material route is added value-added-service contribution and legitimate volume, which remains unquantified.

## Hypotheses, falsifiers and gate view

Strongest positive: lawful network-level context improves fraud decisions across multiple rails and Visa prices the service while preserving more legitimate volume. Strongest negative: rivals and banks reproduce the scoring, contractual data limits weaken pooled learning, and incentives/regulation pass the benefit to clients.

Falsifiers: (1) matched issuer cohorts show no fraud reduction at fixed approval rates; (2) paid attach or renewal stalls while fraud tools remain bundled; (3) A2A share grows while Visa's protection revenue cannot offset card-fee bypass. Unresolved: exact training rights, paid adoption denominator, error-rate protocol, model-serving cost and retained fee.

Confidence: mechanism **high**; deployment **medium**; shareholder capture **low**. G0 candidate: met. G1 qualified business: provisionally met, with litigation/regulation monitored. G2 operating thesis: **provisional, limited** because E2 exists but controlled evidence and material cash bridge do not. G3/G4: deferred by user.
