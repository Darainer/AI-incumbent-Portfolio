## Provisional conclusion

Linde has a durable conventional franchise based on on-site plants, pipeline density, application know-how and long-term customer contracts. The public evidence supports specific AI capabilities and, in some cases, management-reported operational use. It does not provide a controlled, quality-adjusted company result tied to retained incremental cash. The central underwriting issue is capture: operational improvement may pass to customers, be absorbed by implementation, already sit in the reported baseline, or be dominated by cycle and mix. The dossier therefore keeps G2 provisional and treats E3/E4 as unestablished.

## Conventional business and financial anchor

Linde reports Americas, EMEA, APAC and Engineering; on-site plants, merchant liquid and packaged gases serve healthcare and industrial customers. Customer value arises from dependable execution, integration and domain expertise, not data volume alone. Competitive advantage must survive Air Liquide and industrial-control vendors and customer-owned optimization teams. Switching costs and distribution help, but contracts, interoperability and procurement can reallocate gains.

For FY2024, the opened company reporting identifies **$33.005bn sales**, **$8.227bn reported operating profit**, and **$9.4bn operating cash flow and about $4.4bn capital expenditure**. The capital context is that net debt was about $20bn scale. Reported and adjusted measures are not interchangeable: the materiality bridge below uses the explicitly labelled profit measure or proxy and does not present it as normalized earnings. Acquisitions, currency, product/geographic mix, restructuring, commodity or equipment cycles, and accounting changes can confound comparisons. The latest annual-report/results hub should be reopened for exact statement lines during lead review.

The [Q2 2026 issuer release](https://www.linde.com/news-and-media/2026/linde-reports-second-quarter-2026-results) reports quarterly sales of $9.289bn, reported operating profit of $2.554bn, adjusted operating profit of $2.744bn and operating cash flow of $2.271bn. These quarterly figures are the latest update and are not annualized; the 5% scale bridge remains anchored to the completed FY2024 reported measure.

The ordinary baseline already contains deployed analytics, automation and machine learning. Capital allocation must fund maintenance, growth investment, obligations and shareholder distributions before any incremental AI benefit. Financial scale permits experimentation, but it does not demonstrate quality, adoption or capture.

## Measurement contract and retained economics

Pre-register **energy consumed per saleable tonne at fixed purity, plant availability and distribution cost per delivered unit**. Compare randomized or carefully matched sites, clients, products or cohorts against the current production system, including existing ML. Define the eligible workload before rollout; hold service, safety, accuracy, risk and customer-mix quality constant. Report sample size, duration, confidence interval, adoption, exception/rework rate and all human/model/implementation cost. The known public result is insufficient for E3. A future annual report, product cohort study or customer renewal disclosure linking the KPI to contribution could close the gap.

The framework's five-year research convention is **$411m** for this packet. 0.05×$8.227bn=$411m. Illustratively, a $4.0bn eligible operating-cost pool × 8% efficiency × 60% realization × 50% retention − $50m recurring AI cost = $46m, only 11% of the hurdle; customer energy savings are excluded. Assumption inputs are illustrative and reproducible. They are neither guidance nor valuation. Released time counts only if removed or redeployed to valued output; the same capacity cannot be counted as both cost saving and revenue. Capital, working capital and tax would need separate treatment.

## Competing hypotheses, falsifiers and gates

**Strongest positive hypothesis:** proprietary workflow context and distribution let Linde deploy validated AI faster than standalone vendors, with better feedback and a retained service, pricing, throughput or capital benefit. **Strongest negative hypothesis:** model capability commoditizes, customer rights prevent pooling, implementation and verification absorb gains, and Air Liquide or industrial-control vendors and customer-owned optimization teams forces pass-through.

Three falsifiers are: (1) matched deployments fail to improve energy consumed per saleable tonne at fixed purity, plant availability and distribution cost per delivered unit at equal quality; (2) five-year retained benefit after recurring AI and implementation cost remains below the stated 5% hurdle; (3) contracts, portability or competitor performance show that the alleged data/workflow advantage is readily replicated. Unresolved questions are eligible-workload share, exact production penetration, customer-level retention/pricing, recurring model cost, contract training rights and the latest post-FY2024 adverse development.

G0 passes because asset, mechanism, capture route and falsifiers are explicit. G1 is provisionally supported by the conventional franchise and financial capacity, subject to latest-period refresh and sector risks. G2 remains **open**: scoped E1/E2 can support further testing, but E3/E4 are absent. G3 and G4 are **deferred by user**. Confidence is **medium** for mechanism, **low-to-medium** for deployment breadth and **low** for capture.
