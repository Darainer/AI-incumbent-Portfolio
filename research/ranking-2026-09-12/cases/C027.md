## Incremental AI and valuation discipline

As with Visa, prevented merchant/bank fraud is not Mastercard revenue. A 4% terminal earnings addition must come from net network fees and services after issuer incentives, processing costs and competition. It is an upper-case capture assumption, not a measured conversion of network loss savings.

At the stated quote, the base case returns **8.0%**. The 10% base entry is **519.25 USD**, versus 568.54 at 8% and 475.04 at 12%. Holding base multiple/dividends fixed requires **12.1%** ordinary earnings growth for 10% investor IRR.

In the bull case, the separate AI increment adds 0.88 percentage points of annual IRR. The remainder comes from the conventional earnings path, starting price, multiple and dividends; the whole bull return cannot be attributed to AI.

Base IRR falls to 5.8% with a 10% lower earnings anchor, 3.3% with a 20% lower terminal multiple, and 6.0% with ordinary growth 2 points lower.

## Limits and what must hold

Comparable network capture logic and strong conventional economics; thinner verified incremental deployment evidence lowers conviction.

- Value-added services growth includes acquisitions and cannot all be assigned to AI.
- The 28 x base multiple is itself a high-quality-franchise assumption; a 24 x endpoint materially reduces returns.

These local-currency equity returns exclude investor tax, fees and FX. Interest is already in earnings; debt is not deducted again. SBC remains an economic cost. Scenario downside is not a loss floor.
