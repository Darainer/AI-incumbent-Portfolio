## Incremental AI and valuation discipline

AI uplift is a capped bull option for net retained operational savings; see scenario rationale. No AI-driven change in clinical success or insurance risk is modeled.

At the stated quote, the base case returns **3.2%**. The 10% base entry is **101.99 USD**, versus 111.05 at 8% and 93.85 at 12%. Holding base multiple/dividends fixed requires **10.4%** ordinary earnings growth for 10% investor IRR.

In the bull case, the separate AI increment adds 0.60 percentage points of annual IRR. The remainder comes from the conventional earnings path, starting price, multiple and dividends; the whole bull return cannot be attributed to AI.

Base IRR falls to 1.3% with a 10% lower earnings anchor, -0.8% with a 20% lower terminal multiple, and 1.4% with ordinary growth 2 points lower.

## Limits and what must hold

Research workflows and proprietary science offer optionality; acquisition debt and clinical setbacks dominate current cash-risk assessment.

- After September failed primary endpoints, prior mid-term sales goals are not reliable valuation inputs without a fresh asset bridge.
- Net debt 39.4 bn makes repeated large acquisitions or unchanged buybacks harder to fund; 15% reinvestment reserve could be insufficient.
- USD dividend depends on CHF translation and withholding; model presents gross shareholder return.
- Exact rolling diluted share count and current full gross-debt/cash/lease schedule not independently extracted; company net debt and earnings interest burden are verified.
- No evidence warrants changed phase-transition rates from AI; all optionality conditional.

These local-currency equity returns exclude investor tax, fees and FX. Interest is already in earnings; debt is not deducted again. SBC remains an economic cost. Scenario downside is not a loss floor.
