## Incremental AI and valuation discipline

Only 3% terminal upside from AI because client bargaining and fewer billable hours can absorb productivity. At present-scale net profit~USD 8 bn, 240 m net retention would require measurable contract contribution after training/model expense; bookings are not recognized profit.

At the stated quote, the base case returns **10.6%**. The 10% base entry is **188.55 USD**, versus 205.58 at 8% and 173.25 at 12%. Holding base multiple/dividends fixed requires **3.4%** ordinary earnings growth for 10% investor IRR.

In the bull case, the separate AI increment adds 0.65 percentage points of annual IRR. The remainder comes from the conventional earnings path, starting price, multiple and dividends; the whole bull return cannot be attributed to AI.

Base IRR falls to 8.5% with a 10% lower earnings anchor, 6.2% with a 20% lower terminal multiple, and 8.6% with ordinary growth 2 points lower.

## Limits and what must hold

Client AI demand is real, but productivity can reduce billable effort; price may compensate for that risk without proving an AI moat.

- Guidance-to-reported-results risk remains.
- A cheap services franchise can remain cheap if AI structurally lowers billable demand; this is a conventional-value candidate with contested AI capture.

These local-currency equity returns exclude investor tax, fees and FX. Interest is already in earnings; debt is not deducted again. SBC remains an economic cost. Scenario downside is not a loss floor.
