## Incremental AI and valuation discipline

AI uplift is a capped bull option for net retained operational savings; see scenario rationale. No AI-driven change in clinical success or insurance risk is modeled.

At the stated quote, the base case returns **4.2%**. The 10% base entry is **260.27 USD**, versus 284.60 at 8% and 238.45 at 12%. Holding base multiple/dividends fixed requires **11.0%** ordinary earnings growth for 10% investor IRR.

In the bull case, the separate AI increment adds 0.84 percentage points of annual IRR. The remainder comes from the conventional earnings path, starting price, multiple and dividends; the whole bull return cannot be attributed to AI.

Base IRR falls to 2.1% with a 10% lower earnings anchor, -0.1% with a 20% lower terminal multiple, and 2.3% with ordinary growth 2 points lower.

## Limits and what must hold

Underwriting and process ownership provide an internal expense route; normalize catastrophes, reserves and capital before crediting savings.

- Half of prior-year reserve releases may still be too generous if casualty liabilities are understated; reserve development and catastrophe uncertainty dominate small AI savings.
- Q 2 underlying current-accident-year ex-cat combined ratio 82.2% vs 82.3% prior year does not establish AI underwriting improvement.
- No consolidated regulatory solvency ratio was extracted, but audited subsidiary capital/surplus and available distributions are verified; no unsupported excess-capital assumption.
- Core EPS removes Bermuda DTA amortization and integration/severance. Repeated integration expense, alternative-return cyclicality or higher taxes may reduce distributable earnings.
- AI workforce/expense targets remain intentions; no measured retained cash bridge justifies base incremental AI.

These local-currency equity returns exclude investor tax, fees and FX. Interest is already in earnings; debt is not deducted again. SBC remains an economic cost. Scenario downside is not a loss floor.
