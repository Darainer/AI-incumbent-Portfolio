# Platform earnings normalization — 12 September 2026

For the five-year scenarios, use normalized annual EPS of **$16.63 Microsoft, $9.67 Alphabet, $6.83 Amazon and $27.88 Meta**. These retain SBC and ordinary depreciation. They remove investment gains and replace the full reported tax provision with an explicit ordinary-tax assumption. The corresponding P/Es at the root-supplied quotes are approximately **29.8x, 35.0x, 37.6x and 23.2x**. Feed EPS/P/E would materially misstate the starting economics.

All four carry **EPS_VALUATION_CONDITIONAL_CAPITAL_RECOVERY**. Public figures support a historical cash check; they do not establish maintenance capital requirements or sustainable owner cash. Current cash capex substantially exceeds PP&E depreciation, with additional finance leases, unpaid equipment and future capacity commitments. A higher future P/E must be supported by future returns on this capital.

## Bounded five-year scenario inputs

Every growth rate, terminal multiple and incremental-AI change below is an **analyst assumption**, not a verified forecast. Ordinary EPS growth includes continuation of already deployed AI and the current businesses. The separate AI factor is a one-time change in the terminal EPS level beyond that ordinary path. It is not an additional annual growth rate. All EPS growth is net of ongoing SBC, dilution, taxes and funding costs.

Formula: `terminal EPS = normalized starting EPS × (1 + ordinary growth)^5 × (1 + incremental AI level change)`; terminal price is terminal EPS × terminal P/E. Add five cash dividends without reinvestment, then calculate the five-year CAGR. No extra buyback yield, investment revaluation gain, cash balance or holdings NAV is added. Net interest is retained; adding all cash on top would require removing its earnings first.

Current annual common dividends assumed: Microsoft $3.64, Alphabet $0.88, Amazon $0, Meta $2.10. Downside dividends stay flat; base dividend growth is 5%, 5%, 0% and 3%; bull growth is 8%, 8%, 0% and 5%. Future dividends are assumptions. Microsoft’s $0.91 quarter is in its FY2026 10-K, Alphabet’s $0.22 quarter in Q2 results, and Meta’s $0.525 quarter in Q2 10-Q.

The ordinary-growth ranges reflect mature profit pools plus existing cloud growth, constrained by reinvestment. Microsoft receives the highest base multiple because of recurring enterprise distribution. Alphabet’s lower base multiple allows for search monetization risk, new financing and high capex; it also begins around 35x normalized earnings. Amazon’s higher operating-growth range allows margin expansion but its negative cash screen makes the capital hurdle strongest. Meta’s growth and multiple ranges retain Reality Labs and legal costs and allow for a faster depreciation/compensation burden.

The incremental-AI caps are intentionally small: Microsoft and Amazon 0%/5%/10%, Alphabet −5%/0%/5%, Meta −5%/3%/7%. They represent retained terminal contribution after inference, labor, depreciation and ordinary financing. Negative cases permit cannibalization or added costs. These caps are not estimates of causal AI revenue. Do not combine them with a second dollar AI benefit model unless one replaces the other.

Positive base returns remain conditional on capital recovery. The low/negative base return for Alphabet follows from the normalized starting valuation and the conservative scenario, not a prediction that Alphabet’s operating business will shrink. The disclosed inputs let the root substitute other growth/multiple judgments without reusing distorted feed earnings.
