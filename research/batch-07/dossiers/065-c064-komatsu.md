## Provisional conclusion

Komatsu has a durable conventional franchise based on machine installed base, dealers, Smart Construction workflows, fleet telemetry and site knowledge. The public evidence supports specific AI capabilities and, in some cases, management-reported operational use. It does not provide a controlled, quality-adjusted company result tied to retained incremental cash. The central underwriting issue is capture: operational improvement may pass to customers, be absorbed by implementation, already sit in the reported baseline, or be dominated by cycle and mix. The dossier therefore keeps G2 provisional and treats E3/E4 as unestablished.

## Conventional business and financial anchor

Komatsu reports Construction, Mining and Utility Equipment; Retail Finance; and Industrial Machinery. Customer value arises from dependable execution, integration and domain expertise, not data volume alone. Competitive advantage must survive Caterpillar and autonomy specialists and equipment-agnostic fleet-management platforms. Switching costs and distribution help, but contracts, interoperability and procurement can reallocate gains.

For FY2025 ended 31 March 2025, the opened company reporting identifies **about ¥4.1tn net sales**, **about ¥0.66tn operating income**, and **operating cash flow around ¥0.6tn scale with heavy capital and rental-fleet investment**. The capital context is that equipment-finance receivables and borrowings must be separated from industrial operations. Reported and adjusted measures are not interchangeable: the materiality bridge below uses the explicitly labelled profit measure or proxy and does not present it as normalized earnings. Acquisitions, currency, product/geographic mix, restructuring, commodity or equipment cycles, and accounting changes can confound comparisons. The latest annual-report/results hub should be reopened for exact statement lines during lead review.

The [FY ended 31 March 2026 issuer results](https://www.komatsu.jp/en/ir/library/results/) report record net sales, operating income of ¥567.3bn (down 13.7%) and net income attributable to Komatsu of ¥376.4bn (down 14.4%). The next quarter reported operating income of ¥151.6bn. Komatsu’s named production evidence is its commercial Autonomous Haulage System and Smart Construction platform; deployment is E2 as an offered, operating customer workflow, while no controlled tonnes-per-hour or retained-cash comparison was found.

The ordinary baseline already contains deployed analytics, automation and machine learning. Capital allocation must fund maintenance, growth investment, obligations and shareholder distributions before any incremental AI benefit. Financial scale permits experimentation, but it does not demonstrate quality, adoption or capture.

## Measurement contract and retained economics

Pre-register **tonnes or cubic metres per machine-hour at equal safety/quality, idle time, fuel per unit, paid digital renewal and warranty/service contribution**. Compare randomized or carefully matched sites, clients, products or cohorts against the current production system, including existing ML. Define the eligible workload before rollout; hold service, safety, accuracy, risk and customer-mix quality constant. Report sample size, duration, confidence interval, adoption, exception/rework rate and all human/model/implementation cost. The known public result is insufficient for E3. A future annual report, product cohort study or customer renewal disclosure linking the KPI to contribution could close the gap.

The framework's five-year research convention is **¥28.4bn** for this packet. 5%×FY2026 operating income of ¥567.3bn=¥28.365bn. A ¥300bn eligible cost pool×10%×60%×50%−¥10bn=−¥1bn; customer fuel/labor savings cannot be treated as Komatsu savings, so pricing, service and equipment pull-through must carry capture. Assumption inputs are illustrative and reproducible. They are neither guidance nor valuation. Released time counts only if removed or redeployed to valued output; the same capacity cannot be counted as both cost saving and revenue. Capital, working capital and tax would need separate treatment.

## Competing hypotheses, falsifiers and gates

**Strongest positive hypothesis:** proprietary workflow context and distribution let Komatsu deploy validated AI faster than standalone vendors, with better feedback and a retained service, pricing, throughput or capital benefit. **Strongest negative hypothesis:** model capability commoditizes, customer rights prevent pooling, implementation and verification absorb gains, and Caterpillar or autonomy specialists and equipment-agnostic fleet-management platforms forces pass-through.

Three falsifiers are: (1) matched deployments fail to improve tonnes or cubic metres per machine-hour at equal safety/quality, idle time, fuel per unit, paid digital renewal and warranty/service contribution at equal quality; (2) five-year retained benefit after recurring AI and implementation cost remains below the stated 5% hurdle; (3) contracts, portability or competitor performance show that the alleged data/workflow advantage is readily replicated. Unresolved questions are eligible-workload share, exact production penetration, customer-level retention/pricing, recurring model cost, contract training rights and the latest post-June 2026 adverse development.

G0 passes because asset, mechanism, capture route and falsifiers are explicit. G1 is provisionally supported by the conventional franchise and financial capacity, subject to latest-period refresh and sector risks. G2 remains **open**: scoped E1/E2 can support further testing, but E3/E4 are absent. G3 and G4 are **deferred by user**. Confidence is **medium** for mechanism, **low-to-medium** for deployment breadth and **low** for capture.
