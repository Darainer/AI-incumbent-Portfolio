## Provisional conclusion

DHL Group has a durable conventional franchise based on shipment density, hubs, depots, contracts and route-level operating history. The public evidence supports specific AI capabilities and, in some cases, management-reported operational use. It does not provide a controlled, quality-adjusted company result tied to retained incremental cash. The central underwriting issue is capture: operational improvement may pass to customers, be absorbed by implementation, already sit in the reported baseline, or be dominated by cycle and mix. The dossier therefore keeps G2 provisional and treats E3/E4 as unestablished.

## Conventional business and financial anchor

DHL Group reports Express, Global Forwarding/Freight, Supply Chain, eCommerce and Post & Parcel Germany. Customer value arises from dependable execution, integration and domain expertise, not data volume alone. Competitive advantage must survive UPS and digital freight brokers and carrier-neutral optimization software. Switching costs and distribution help, but contracts, interoperability and procurement can reallocate gains.

For FY2025, company reporting identifies **about €82.9bn revenue** and **€6.1bn EBIT**. The pre-tax operating metric governs the hurdle; leases, pensions and net debt remain material in this network business.

The [H1 2026 reporting hub](https://reporting-hub.group.dhl.com/2026-fy/en/half-year-report/results-of-operations/) reports group revenue of €42.787bn, up from €40.634bn, including €656m adverse currency effect. FY2025 revenue was about €82.9bn and EBIT €6.1bn. The group’s AI trend report documents use cases, but no controlled route/load cohort tied to retained EBIT was found.

The ordinary baseline already contains deployed analytics, automation and machine learning. Capital allocation must fund maintenance, growth investment, obligations and shareholder distributions before any incremental AI benefit. Financial scale permits experimentation, but it does not demonstrate quality, adoption or capture.

## Measurement contract and retained economics

Pre-register **cost per delivered unit at fixed on-time/damage service, empty miles, stop density, asset utilization and retained margin**. Compare randomized or carefully matched sites, clients, products or cohorts against the current production system, including existing ML. Define the eligible workload before rollout; hold service, safety, accuracy, risk and customer-mix quality constant. Report sample size, duration, confidence interval, adoption, exception/rework rate and all human/model/implementation cost. The known public result is insufficient for E3. A future annual report, product cohort study or customer renewal disclosure linking the KPI to contribution could close the gap.

The framework's annual research convention is **€305m** for this packet: 5% × FY2025 EBIT of €6.1bn, on a pre-tax operating basis. A €10bn eligible operating-cost pool×5%×60%×40%−€100m=€20m. Wage agreements, customer pricing and service quality determine realization; shipper savings are excluded. Assumption inputs are illustrative and reproducible.

## Competing hypotheses, falsifiers and gates

**Strongest positive hypothesis:** proprietary workflow context and distribution let DHL Group deploy validated AI faster than standalone vendors, with better feedback and a retained service, pricing, throughput or capital benefit. **Strongest negative hypothesis:** model capability commoditizes, customer rights prevent pooling, implementation and verification absorb gains, and UPS or digital freight brokers and carrier-neutral optimization software forces pass-through.

Three falsifiers are: (1) matched deployments fail to improve cost per delivered unit at fixed on-time/damage service, empty miles, stop density, asset utilization and retained margin at equal quality; (2) five-year retained benefit after recurring AI and implementation cost remains below the stated 5% hurdle; (3) contracts, portability or competitor performance show that the alleged data/workflow advantage is readily replicated. Unresolved questions are eligible-workload share, exact production penetration, customer-level retention/pricing, recurring model cost, contract training rights and the latest post-H1 2026 adverse development.

G0 passes because asset, mechanism, capture route and falsifiers are explicit. G1 is provisionally supported by the conventional franchise and financial capacity, subject to latest-period refresh and sector risks. G2 remains **open**: scoped E1/E2 can support further testing, but E3/E4 are absent. G3 and G4 are **deferred by user**. Confidence is **medium** for mechanism, **low-to-medium** for deployment breadth and **low** for capture.
