# Visa: initial diligence brief

**ID:** C026 · **Area:** [Payments and transaction networks](../../industries/f04-payments-and-transaction-networks.md) · **Evidence:** E1 · **Status:** research only

## Thesis to test

Network fraud signals and merchant-issuer connectivity. AI can identify fraud and reduce false declines using context that individual merchants or issuers cannot easily reproduce.

## Valuation debate

Can AI risk services add profit beyond an already valued network moat? This is a question to investigate, not a claim about the current share price. Price, consensus, valuation and expected return have not been assessed.

## Observed evidence

- [Visa Protect](https://www.visa.com/en-us/solutions/visa-protect): Offers AI-supported authentication, authorization and payment-fraud decision services. Product availability alone does not establish net fraud reduction or network shareholder capture.

## Decisive evidence request

Fraud and approval lift with net service fees. Require production scope, baseline, comparison group and a bridge to cash earnings. Compare the same task against Mastercard, processors and account-to-account providers. Quantify whether the incumbent's unique assets improve the result after accounting for model access and total cost.

## Principal risk and disconfirmation

Account-to-account alternatives and fee regulation. Area-level falsifier: Fraud shifts rather than falls, network fees compress, or account-to-account rails bypass the service.

## Provisional assessment

Structural prior: **98/100**, entirely a hypothesis rather than a validated result. Asset 5/5, AI leverage 5/5, feedback 5/5, testability 4/5, distribution 5/5, capture 5/5. See [dimension rationales](../../universe/score-rationales.csv).

Management execution, data usability and financial resilience require separate evidence. Current downside valuation and AI contribution remain unknown. Confidence is low in a purchase decision until these gaps close.
