# Valuation and retained productivity

## The central separation

A good AI beneficiary can be an expensive stock. A cheap incumbent can be structurally impaired. The universe is deliberately price-independent; no current price or fair value has been populated. The initial 18-name queue asks where productivity might be underappreciated. It does not establish that it is.

## Conventional business first

Build a dated base valuation without incremental AI benefits beyond those already embedded in reported economics. Record reporting currency, price timestamp, listing, share class, diluted shares, debt, cash, leases, minority interests, pensions where material, and the treatment of stock-based compensation. Normalize cyclicality and one-offs.

For operating businesses, reconcile enterprise value and equity value using cash flows and capital requirements. For banks and insurers, use equity earnings, required regulatory capital and excess capital consistently. For pharma, use risk-adjusted pipeline and patent-expiry scenarios without counting the same candidate in both the base pipeline and AI option. For REITs, distinguish AFFO, maintenance capex, debt refinancing and tenant pass-through.

## A transparent cost bridge

For cost-oriented applications, define:

`retained annual operating benefit = A × e × r × c − K`

where `A` is the eligible annual cost pool, `e` is measured task efficiency, `r` is the fraction converted into removed cost or valued output, `c` is the fraction retained after competitive/customer/employee sharing, and `K` is recurring AI and implementation expense. Units must be consistent.

Use a separate cash-flow line for incremental capital expenditure and working capital, and apply tax consistently. Do not subtract capital expenditure twice through both expense and cash investment. If valuing extra output instead of removed cost, use incremental contribution margin on that output; do not also count the same released capacity as labor savings.

## Illustrative sensitivity, not a forecast

Assume revenue of 100 monetary units, operating cost of 80 and operating profit of 20. Eligible cost is 40. Recurring AI expense is 1. These are invented round numbers solely to expose the mechanism.

| Scenario | Task efficiency | Realization | Retained share | Gross task saving | Net operating benefit | New operating profit |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Weak capture | 15% | 40% | 25% | 6.0 | -0.4 | 19.6 |
| Central illustration | 15% | 60% | 50% | 6.0 | 0.8 | 20.8 |
| Strong capture | 25% | 80% | 75% | 10.0 | 5.0 | 25.0 |

A 15% task improvement can therefore produce only a 4% increase in operating profit in the central illustration. It can also destroy value after cost. Conversely, additional growth or decision quality may matter more than cost savings. Each requires its own non-overlapping model.

## Growth and decision-quality paths

For payments, model legitimate transaction volume and net service fees after fraud costs and rebates. For insurance, model risk-adjusted losses and expenses without assuming that lower observed claims mean better underwriting. For software, subtract seat cannibalization and model-serving cost from incremental subscription or outcome revenue. For pharma, explicitly change stage-specific transition probabilities only when evidence supports them, then account for time, costs and commercial competition.

## Reverse valuation and margin of safety

Solve for the growth, margins, capital turnover or pipeline success embedded in the current price. Compare those requirements with the conventional-business case and explicit AI scenarios. Do not call AI upside free because a headline multiple looks low. Lower multiples may reflect deteriorating core economics.

The eventual underpriced shortlist must include a bear case with no incremental AI benefit, a base case with evidence-linked adoption and a bull case with constrained upside. State discount rates, terminal assumptions and horizon, and show sensitivity. Proposed entry values require a return hurdle chosen for the portfolio and risk, not a universal arbitrary discount.
