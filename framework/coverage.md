# Coverage, boundaries and negative controls

The universe spans all 11 broad equity sectors using [MSCI's GICS sector structure](https://www.msci.com/indexes/index-resources/gics) as a checklist. The 36 areas are our own research groupings, not official company classifications. Conglomerates receive one primary area and optional secondary tags. A company appears once, even when several mechanisms apply.

## Areas requiring more granular work

| Specialist area | Provisional mechanism | Research decision and test |
|---|---|---|
| Insurance brokers | Submission and placement productivity | Extend F01. Test revenue per account after client fee renegotiation, not underwriter loss ratios. No dedicated initial candidate. |
| Education and training | Personalized teaching and support | T02/C04 adjacency. Generic content faces substitution. Require learning outcomes and paid retention. No initial inclusion. |
| Staffing and recruiting | Matching candidates to jobs | I07/D05 adjacency. Faster matching may reduce fees. Require placement quality and retained contribution. |
| Restaurants and hotels | Scheduling, demand forecasting and service | C02/S02 adjacency. Franchise contract economics determine capture. Require same-unit profit net of implementation cost. |
| Packaging, cement and building products | Process optimization and design | M01 adjacency. Control energy, utilization and commodity prices. Require retained unit-margin gain. |
| Consumer electronics and hardware | Product features and manufacturing | I05/C03 adjacency. Separate product AI demand from internal productivity. Test paid differentiation against commoditization. |
| Construction and engineering contractors | Estimation, design and site coordination | I04/D05 adjacency. Require project margin after rework and fixed-price risk. |
| Shipping and airlines | Routing, pricing and maintenance | D03/C02 adjacency. Capacity cycles and fuel often dominate. Require cycle-adjusted unit economics. |
| Water utilities and midstream | Leakage detection, maintenance and dispatch | U01/E01 adjacency. Contract/regulatory sharing determines shareholder benefit. |
| Agriculture inputs and timber | Field or forestry optimization | M01/M02/D02 adjacency. Require useful labels, biological validation and actual ownership of outcomes. |
| Data-center REITs | Power/cooling optimization and AI tenant demand | R01. Separate owner-retained efficiency from rent, construction and financing exposure. No initial dedicated data-center owner. |
| Tobacco, gambling and defense | Various design, marketing or process applications | No candidates in this first release. Inclusion would require a separately stated investment mandate and a material mechanism. This is a scope choice, not a claim of technical inapplicability. |

Coverage across sectors is complete at the broad level. Coverage of every specialist subindustry is not. These explicit extensions prevent the first 100 names from being mistaken for an exhaustive investable market.

## Negative controls

Use lower-capture or disruption-exposed candidates to challenge the regime: hourly IT services, price-led grocery, regulated utilities, commodity chemicals, conventional automakers and property owners with tenant pass-through. Their inclusion is intentional. If the framework labels every incumbent a winner, it has failed.

The strongest alternative thesis is that cheaper intelligence reduces scale advantages: entrants rent models, agents bypass incumbent interfaces, data becomes portable and customers keep the surplus. Every priority memo must compare this alternative with the incumbent thesis.

## Portfolio implications

A cross-sector list can still concentrate in the same factors: long-duration growth, cloud suppliers, corporate IT budgets, interest rates, regulated data rights or the capital-spending cycle. Secondary tags expose some overlap but do not quantify correlation. This release does not infer diversification from sector counts or propose weights.
