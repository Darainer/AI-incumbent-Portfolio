# AI Incumbent Productivity Regime
## A portfolio framework for identifying established companies that can convert AI into durable improvements in business performance

**Status:** Working investment thesis  
**Purpose:** Cross-industry portfolio regime / screening framework  
**Origin:** Distilled from the Sanofi / pharmaceutical R&D discussion

---

## 1. Core Thesis

AI is likely to create more value than simply reducing headcount or automating administrative work.

The larger opportunity is that AI can make **existing scale, proprietary data, domain expertise, physical infrastructure, customer relationships, and capital substantially more productive**.

This may disproportionately benefit high-quality incumbents that already possess the inputs needed to turn AI into a compounding operating advantage.

The investment thesis is therefore:

> **Identify incumbents whose existing assets become materially more valuable when AI makes those assets searchable, reusable, predictive, and scalable.**

The goal is not to find the company with the most impressive AI model.

The goal is to find companies where AI can improve the economics of an already-strong business.

---

## 2. The Mechanism

The general mechanism can be represented as:

**Scale → proprietary data → better AI decisions → better products / lower costs / higher success rates → more customers / transactions / experiments → more proprietary data → greater scale**

AI may strengthen the value of assets that were already difficult to replicate.

Examples include:

- proprietary historical datasets;
- customer behavior data;
- experimental and failure data;
- engineering and operational telemetry;
- regulatory and compliance history;
- manufacturing process knowledge;
- distribution networks;
- installed equipment bases;
- specialist workflows;
- domain experts;
- capital to run new experiments;
- direct customer relationships.

A company does not need exclusive access to the best foundation model.

If models increasingly commoditize, the differentiating layer may instead become:

> **proprietary data + workflows + domain expertise + execution + feedback loops**

---

## 3. Why Large Incumbents May Benefit Disproportionately

Historically, scale could be both an advantage and a liability.

Large organizations had:

- more capital;
- more data;
- more customers;
- more infrastructure;
- but also more bureaucracy, fragmented knowledge, and slower decision-making.

AI potentially changes this balance.

It can make accumulated organizational knowledge easier to exploit and allow a smaller number of highly capable people to supervise much larger amounts of analysis, experimentation, engineering, and decision-making.

This means an incumbent may not merely save costs.

It may become capable of producing **far more output from the same organizational base**.

The important question is therefore not:

> “How many employees can AI replace?”

It is:

> **“How much more economic output can this organization produce from its existing assets?”**

---

## 4. The Compounding Advantage

The most attractive companies will have a closed feedback loop.

### Generic flywheel

1. The company already possesses a valuable proprietary dataset or installed base.
2. AI improves prediction, selection, design, or decision quality.
3. Better decisions improve product quality, economics, customer outcomes, or capital allocation.
4. The company attracts more customers, runs more experiments, or processes more transactions.
5. These activities generate additional proprietary data.
6. The next generation of AI-assisted decisions becomes better.

This can create **increasing returns to scale** without requiring winner-takes-all market structure.

Multiple large incumbents can improve simultaneously if the underlying market expands.

---

## 5. The Pharma Example

Pharmaceutical R&D illustrates the thesis clearly.

A large pharmaceutical company already possesses:

- decades of clinical trial data;
- failed experiments;
- pharmacokinetic and toxicity data;
- biomarker data;
- genomics;
- patient outcomes;
- manufacturing knowledge;
- regulatory experience;
- scientific talent;
- capital to fund new experiments;
- global trial infrastructure.

AI can potentially improve:

- target selection;
- molecule and antibody design;
- toxicity prediction;
- dose selection;
- patient stratification;
- trial design;
- recruitment;
- regulatory preparation;
- portfolio prioritization;
- licensing and acquisition decisions.

The most valuable outcome may not be reducing a ten-year drug program to two years.

It may instead be:

- killing weak programs earlier;
- increasing the number of high-quality candidates;
- improving Phase II / Phase III success rates;
- reducing late-stage failures;
- increasing R&D throughput per euro invested.

If successful, this turns a pharmaceutical company from a collection of individual drugs into a more productive **drug-generation engine**.

The same logic may apply in other industries.

---

## 6. This Is Not Necessarily Winner-Takes-All

A critical part of the thesis is that several incumbents can win at the same time.

If AI increases productivity across an industry while demand is also expanding, competitors do not necessarily cancel out each other's gains.

Examples:

- better drugs can expand the number of treatable patients;
- better insurance underwriting can expand profitable coverage;
- better industrial automation can expand economically viable production;
- better software can increase the amount of work companies automate;
- better financial data tools can deepen usage across existing customers;
- better engineering tools can increase the number of products companies can design and support.

The relevant structure is often closer to an **oligopoly with rising productivity** than a winner-takes-all platform.

---

## 7. The Portfolio Regime

The portfolio regime should seek companies with four characteristics:

### 7.1 Strong incumbent assets

The company already owns something difficult to reproduce:

- proprietary data;
- distribution;
- installed base;
- brand / trust;
- regulatory licenses;
- physical infrastructure;
- exclusive contracts;
- recurring workflow integration;
- technical IP;
- customer relationships.

### 7.2 High AI leverage

AI can materially improve:

- decision quality;
- R&D productivity;
- pricing;
- underwriting;
- asset utilization;
- engineering output;
- customer service;
- sales efficiency;
- logistics;
- fraud detection;
- product development;
- capital allocation.

### 7.3 Feedback-loop potential

Better AI should generate more data or activity that improves the system again.

This is stronger than a one-off automation project.

### 7.4 Economic capture

The company must be able to retain a meaningful portion of the productivity gain.

Improvement that is immediately competed away, regulated away, or transferred entirely to customers may still benefit society while producing little shareholder value.

## 8. Screening Framework

Score each company from **0 to 5** on the following dimensions.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Proprietary data | Does the company possess high-value data competitors cannot easily reproduce? |
| Data quality | Is the data sufficiently structured, contextualized, and usable? |
| Feedback loop | Does operating the business continuously generate additional proprietary data? |
| AI leverage | Can AI materially improve the core economics rather than peripheral functions? |
| Domain expertise | Does the company employ experts capable of validating and acting on AI outputs? |
| Experiment capability | Can it cheaply test AI-generated hypotheses in the real world? |
| Scale | Does it have enough customers, assets, experiments, or transactions for AI improvements to compound? |
| Capital | Can it fund infrastructure, acquisitions, experimentation, and deployment? |
| Distribution | Can improvements be deployed rapidly across a large customer base? |
| Switching costs / lock-in | Is the customer relationship durable once AI improves the product? |
| Market growth | Is underlying demand expanding enough for several winners? |
| Value capture | Can productivity improvements translate into margins, growth, or ROIC? |
| Management execution | Is management actually deploying AI into workflows rather than marketing it? |
| Valuation | Is the market already pricing in the AI productivity gain? |

### Suggested interpretation

- **55–70:** potential core holding under the regime
- **45–54:** strong watchlist candidate
- **35–44:** interesting, but AI advantage not yet proven
- **<35:** probably not suitable for the regime

The scoring thresholds are heuristic and should be refined with experience.

Potential advantages:

- proprietary clinical and experimental datasets;
- large R&D budgets;
- massive cost of failure;
- high value from better candidate selection;
- strong feedback loops;
- expanding demand for effective treatments.

Key question:

> Which companies convert AI into measurable improvements in R&D success rates?

Potential advantages:

- enormous historical claims datasets;
- pricing and underwriting models;
- fraud data;
- catastrophe modeling;
- portfolio optimization.

AI may improve underwriting quality, claims handling, pricing precision, and risk selection.

Key question:

> Does better prediction improve combined ratios without causing competitors to price away the gain?

---

### Financial information / professional data

Examples include legal, financial, scientific, compliance, and workflow-data businesses.

Potential advantages:

- proprietary structured datasets;
- deeply embedded customer workflows;
- high switching costs;
- AI can convert static information into decision-support systems.

This category may be particularly attractive because AI increases the value of trusted proprietary data.

Key question:

> Does AI strengthen the incumbent's workflow moat or allow generic models to bypass it?

---

### Industrial automation and engineering

Potential advantages:

- machine telemetry;
- installed equipment bases;
- digital twins;
- maintenance histories;

Key question:

> Can the incumbent turn its installed base into an AI-enhanced recurring software / services business?

## 10. What Would Falsify the Thesis?

This regime should not become an excuse to label every incumbent an AI winner.

Important failure modes include:

### Data is not usable

Historical information may be:

- fragmented;
- poorly labeled;
- incompatible;
- biased;
- legally restricted;
- stored in obsolete systems.

Large quantities of bad data do not create an advantage.

### Models commoditize the incumbent's product

AI may reduce barriers to entry rather than increase them.

A proprietary workflow can lose value if a general-purpose model performs the task sufficiently well.

### Organizational inertia

A company may possess exceptional data but fail to reorganize workflows around AI.

Legacy processes, politics, incentives, and management structure can prevent productivity gains.

### Productivity gets competed away

If every competitor obtains the same improvement, prices may decline and customers may capture most of the benefit.

### Regulation captures the surplus

In sectors such as healthcare, banking, utilities, and insurance, governments or regulators may prevent shareholders from capturing the full productivity improvement.

### AI increases capital intensity

Large investments in compute, data engineering, compliance, and infrastructure may offset part of the productivity gain.

### Quality / safety limits adoption

In high-consequence industries, human verification and physical-world testing remain unavoidable.

## 11. Evidence Required Before Increasing Position Size

We should distinguish **AI narrative** from **AI economics**.

The portfolio should reward observable operating improvement.

Useful evidence includes:

### Productivity

- output per employee;
- revenue per employee;
- R&D output per euro;
- engineering throughput;
- lower service cost per customer.

### Decision quality

- lower loss ratios;
- lower failure rates;
- higher conversion;
- lower fraud;
- better pricing accuracy;
- better capital allocation.

### Speed

- shorter development cycles;
- faster onboarding;
- faster product launches;
- lower time-to-decision;
- shorter clinical development timelines.

### Success rate

- improved drug-development success rates;
- fewer failed projects;
- improved product hit rate;
- fewer underwriting errors;
- improved forecast accuracy.

### Economics

- higher ROIC;
- improving operating margins;
- lower incremental cost of growth;
- better retention;
- higher customer lifetime value.

The strongest evidence is when several of these improve simultaneously.

## 12. Valuation Discipline

The most attractive opportunities may be companies where:

1. the incumbent advantage is already strong;
2. AI can materially increase productivity;
3. management is already deploying AI;
4. the market still values the business primarily on its historical economics.

This creates the possibility of buying:

> **a traditional incumbent at a traditional multiple while receiving an AI-driven productivity option for little or no explicit premium.**

Avoid paying extreme valuations merely because a company describes itself as AI-enabled.

The regime is intended to identify **underappreciated productivity optionality**, not chase AI branding.

---

## 13. Portfolio Construction

This should be treated as a **cross-industry regime**, not a single-sector theme.

The preferred structure is:

- multiple industries;
- multiple independent AI use cases;
- established businesses with strong current economics;
- AI as an upside accelerator rather than the sole investment case.

A company should ideally remain investable even if the AI productivity thesis develops more slowly than expected.

### Core principle

> **Buy strong businesses where AI can improve an already-good economic engine. Do not buy weak businesses merely because AI might rescue them.**

---

## 14. Candidate Archetypes

The regime should prioritize several archetypes.

### The Data-Rich Compounder

Owns proprietary information and embeds it in recurring customer workflows.

AI increases the value extracted from the dataset.

### The Scientific Engine

Uses AI to improve R&D selection, experimentation, and development productivity.

### The Installed-Base Optimizer

Owns a large physical or software installed base and uses AI to improve utilization, maintenance, or customer economics.

### The Decision Machine

Processes enormous numbers of high-value decisions—pricing, underwriting, fraud, risk, logistics, allocation—and can use AI to improve each marginal decision.

### The Distribution Giant

Already has customer access and can deploy AI-enabled products broadly at low incremental distribution cost.

### The Regulatory Fortress

Operates in a difficult regulated market where incumbency, data, trust, and compliance infrastructure remain substantial barriers to entry.

The best companies may fit several archetypes simultaneously.

---

## 15. Initial Portfolio Research Question

For every major industry, ask:

> **Which incumbents possess assets that become more valuable as AI improves, and which of those incumbents can convert that improvement into durable shareholder returns?**

Then answer:

1. What proprietary asset does the company possess?
2. Why is it difficult to reproduce?
3. How specifically does AI improve its value?
4. Does usage generate more proprietary data?
5. Can the company experimentally validate AI outputs?
6. Can it deploy improvements across a large installed base?
7. Will customers, competitors, employees, or regulators capture most of the gain?
8. What measurable KPI would prove the thesis?
9. What would falsify the thesis?
10. Is the AI upside already reflected in valuation?

---

## 16. Working Investment Thesis

> **AI is likely to increase the economic value of scale in many industries. Incumbents with proprietary data, domain expertise, capital, distribution, installed infrastructure, and the ability to generate new real-world feedback may use increasingly commoditized AI models to make these accumulated assets substantially more productive. This can create reinforcing feedback loops in which better AI drives better decisions, better products, more activity, more proprietary data, and further AI improvement.**
>
> **The effect need not produce a single winner. In large and growing markets, several incumbents can become more productive simultaneously, reinforcing an oligopoly of strong players. The most attractive investments are companies where AI improves an already high-quality business, the productivity gain can be retained by shareholders, and the market has not yet priced the company as an AI beneficiary.**

Build an **AI Incumbent Productivity Universe** across major sectors.

For each company, record:

- industry;
- proprietary data / asset;
